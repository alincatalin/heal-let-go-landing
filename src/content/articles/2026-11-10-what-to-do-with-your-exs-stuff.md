Their hoodie is still on the chair. There's a toothbrush in the bathroom, a book you never finished and a drawer of photos you can't look at.

Every one of those things is a reason to think about them, and some are a reason to text them. Here's how to deal with all of it without making it worse.

## Should You Give Their Stuff Back?

Yes for anything valuable, practical or personal to them: keys, documents, electronics, family items, clothes they'd miss. Keeping those creates an excuse for contact later ("I still have your charger").

No need for small things they won't miss, like a toothbrush or a takeaway menu. Those can go.

## How to Return Things Without Breaking No Contact

Do it once, and keep it about logistics.

- **Collect everything first.** One box, one trip. Several handovers mean several conversations.
- **Use a go-between if you can.** A friend, a family member or a doorstep drop-off at a time you agree.
- **Keep the message short.** "Your things are in a box. I can leave it with Alex on Saturday, or post it. Which works?"
- **Don't put a note in the box.** If you want to say something, write it somewhere it won't be sent, like our free [unsent text tool](/tools/unsent-text/).
- **Ask for your things back the same way**, in the same message. Then let anything small go.

Returning belongings is one of the few kinds of contact that doesn't break no contact, as long as it stays practical. More in [what counts as breaking no contact](/blog/articles/what-counts-as-breaking-no-contact/).

## What to Do With Everything Else

| Item | What to do now | Later |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Their valuables, keys, documents | Return, once | Done |
| Their clothes | Return, or donate if they don't want them back | Done |
| Gifts they gave you | Box it if it hurts to see | Keep, sell or donate once it doesn't sting |
| Photos and printed memories | Box them, out of sight | Decide in a few months |
| Photos on your phone | Move them to a hidden album or archive | Delete or keep when you're calmer |
| Shared things you both used | Keep what you'd have bought anyway | Replace the rest slowly |
| Jewelry or expensive gifts | Keep for now, box it | Return or sell if you want; there's no rule |

## The Box Method

Not sure what to keep? Don't decide now. Put everything that hurts into one box and put it somewhere you don't see every day: a closet, a high shelf, a friend's garage.

Breakups make everything feel urgent, including throwing things away. Deleting every photo in the first week can feel good on the day and sad a year later. A box gives you distance now and choice later.

Set a reminder for three months from now. Open the box then and decide what you want to keep. It's often easier than you expect, and some things you were sure you'd throw out turn out to be fine to keep.

## What About Their Smell, Songs and Your Space?

Some reminders aren't objects. A pillow that smells like them, a playlist, the side of the bed.

- Wash the bedding. Swap which side you sleep on for a while.
- Make a new playlist of your own and leave the shared ones alone for a while.
- Move one piece of furniture or redecorate one corner. Making a space feel like yours helps more than people expect.

If you shared a home for a long time, our guide to [getting over a long-term relationship](/blog/articles/how-to-get-over-a-long-term-relationship/) has more on rebuilding your space and routines.

## FAQ: Your Ex's Stuff

**Should I keep gifts from my ex?**
You can. Gifts are yours. If they hurt to see, box them for now. Some people return expensive gifts like jewelry, but there's no rule that you have to.

**Is it okay to throw away my ex's things?**
For their belongings, offer to return them first, then donate or throw away what they don't want after a reasonable time. For your own reminders, it's your choice, but waiting a few months before throwing away photos and letters avoids regret.

**What if my ex won't come and get their stuff?**
Give a clear deadline in one message, like two weeks, and offer to post it or leave it with someone. After that, you can donate it.

**Should I delete photos of my ex?**
Not right away. Move them to a hidden album or archive so they're out of your feed and camera roll, and decide later when it hurts less.
