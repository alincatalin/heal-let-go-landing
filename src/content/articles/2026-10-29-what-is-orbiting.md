They haven't texted in three weeks. But every time you post a story, their name is near the top of the viewer list. Sometimes there's a like. Never a message.

That's orbiting.

## What Does Orbiting Mean?

Orbiting means someone pulls out of a relationship or talking stage but stays close on social media. They don't talk to you. They just keep circling: watching, liking, reacting.

Researchers describe it the same way. A 2021 study of 208 young adults says orbiting "is identical to ghosting, but the disengager still visibly follows the former partner on social media" ([Pancani et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/02654075211000417)). A 2022 follow-up puts it as "periodically following the victims on SNSs, without starting any direct communication with them" ([Pancani et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.5817/CP2022-2-9)).

The key part is "visibly." Stories and likes show your name to the other person. So whether they mean to or not, the orbiter is sending a signal, and you're the one left trying to read it.

## Signs Someone Is Orbiting You

- They stopped replying or went quiet, but still watch your stories, often quickly
- They like old or new posts without saying anything
- They react to a story with an emoji, then don't follow up
- They follow you on a new platform after the breakup
- If you message them, the reply is short or never comes
- You find yourself checking your viewer list for their name

## Orbiting vs Ghosting vs Breadcrumbing vs Hoovering

| | What they do | Do they talk to you? | What it does to you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Orbiting | Stop talking but keep watching and liking your posts | No | Keeps you guessing if they still care |
| Ghosting | Disappear with no explanation, online and off | No | Leaves you confused and without closure |
| [Breadcrumbing](/blog/articles/what-is-breadcrumbing/) | Send small bits of attention but never commit | A little, now and then | Keeps you hoping and waiting |
| Hoovering | Come back with a big push to pull you back in | Yes, suddenly a lot | Restarts the cycle |

These can overlap. An ex can orbit for a month, then send a breadcrumb, then go quiet again.

## Why Does My Ex Keep Watching My Stories?

Often for small reasons. Your story comes up in their feed and they tap through it. They're curious how you're doing. They miss you a bit but don't want to talk. They want to keep you in view without the work of a real conversation. Sometimes they want you to see that they're watching.

The honest answer is that a story view can't tell you which one it is. It takes one tap, so it carries very little meaning. We wrote more about that in [what is he thinking during no contact](/blog/articles/what-is-he-thinking-during-no-contact/): their silence and their activity online are snapshots, not a summary of how they feel.

What a view isn't is a plan to come back. If they wanted to talk, they would message you.

## Why Does Orbiting Hurt?

Because it leaves the door half open. You can't grieve the relationship as over, and you can't treat it as alive.

The research gives a mixed picture. In the 2021 study, people who had been ghosted or orbited went through roughly four stages: "surprise and confusion," then "anger, sadness, and guilt," then "attempts of relational repair," and finally "acceptance." In the 2022 study of 176 adults, being ghosted was worse than being clearly rejected, and orbiting sat in between: the ambiguous signals "seemed to buffer the victims partially" from the effects of the breakup.

So orbiting may sting a little less than being dropped completely in the moment. The cost comes from what you do with it. Checking an ex's profile is linked to more distress and longing for them, and less personal growth after a breakup, in a study of 464 people ([Marshall, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1089/cyber.2012.0125)). Orbiting gives you a reason to keep checking. If you're stuck on that, see [how to stop checking your ex's Instagram](/blog/articles/stop-checking-ex-instagram/).

## What to Do When an Ex Is Orbiting You

**1. Name it.** "They're watching, not talking." Saying it plainly takes away some of the mystery.

**2. Stop checking the viewer list.** This is the habit that keeps orbiting working. If you can't stop, make it harder: post less for a while, or post to close friends only.

**3. Mute, hide or remove them.** You can hide your stories from one person or mute theirs, so you stop seeing each other. Removing or blocking is fine too. Pick what you can stick to.

**4. Don't reply to a like or an emoji.** A reaction is not an opening. Answering it tells them the low-effort version of you is enough.

**5. Don't post for them.** If you catch yourself choosing a photo because they'll see it, that's a sign to step back from posting.

**6. Treat it like [no contact](/blog/articles/no-contact-rule-guide/).** Watching their profile or posting at them isn't technically contact, but it keeps the same loop going. See [what counts as breaking no contact](/blog/articles/what-counts-as-breaking-no-contact/).

## FAQ: Orbiting

**Does orbiting mean they want me back?**
Not on its own. A view or a like takes one second and can mean curiosity, boredom or habit. Someone who wants you back will message you and make a plan, not just watch.

**Is orbiting a form of manipulation?**
Sometimes it's done on purpose to stay on your mind. Often it's just low-effort curiosity. Either way, you can step back without working out which.

**Should I block someone who is orbiting me?**
You can, if seeing their name keeps pulling you back. Muting them or hiding your stories from them works too, and feels less final. What matters is that you stop seeing each other's updates.

**What's the difference between orbiting and ghosting?**
Both stop talking to you. A ghost disappears completely. An orbiter stays visible on social media, watching and liking, which keeps you wondering.

**Should I watch their stories back?**
Try not to. Watching back keeps you both in the loop and gives you new things to read into. Muting their stories is the kinder choice for you.
