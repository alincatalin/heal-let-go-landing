"Heartbreak" sounds like a metaphor until you're in it. Then it's a tight chest, a stomach that won't take food, waking at 4am with your heart pounding, and an ache that seems to sit in your body.

That's not you being dramatic. It's what heartbreak does. And like any injury, it heals better when you stop reopening it.

## How Do You Heal a Broken Heart?

**Treat it like an injury, because your brain treats it like one.** You can't speed up the healing itself, but you can stop the things that keep reopening the wound and support the things that close it.

| What heartbreak does | Why it happens | What helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical ache, tight chest | Rejection activates brain regions that also process physical pain | Rest, warmth, movement, time |
| Craving them, checking your phone | Rejection lights up reward and craving systems, like withdrawal | No contact, mute them everywhere |
| Racing thoughts, replaying the end | Your mind keeps looking for an answer or a way back | Write it down, then put it away |
| Not sleeping, not eating | Stress hormones run high in the first weeks | Small regular meals, a wind-down routine |
| Feeling like you don't know who you are | Part of your identity was "us" | Rebuild one thing that's yours each week |
| Sudden waves of grief | Reminders trigger the loss all over again | Let the wave come, have a plan for it |

The rest of this guide goes through what to do, in the order you'll need it.

## Why Does a Broken Heart Hurt Physically?

Because your brain uses some of the same wiring for both. In a brain-scan study, 40 people who had recently been through a breakup they didn't want looked at a photo of their ex, and in another task felt painful heat on their arm. The two experiences activated overlapping regions involved in physical pain ([Kross et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1102693108)).

A second study scanned people who had just been rejected but were still in love. Seeing their ex activated reward and craving areas linked to addiction ([Fisher et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00784.2009)). That's why you can know they're bad for you and still ache for them. Part of heartbreak is withdrawal.

There's good news in the same study: the longer it had been since the rejection, the less active one of those reward areas was. The craving does fade.

## The First 72 Hours: Protect the Wound

In the first few days, healing isn't the goal. Getting through each hour without making it worse is.

- **Don't contact them.** Not to ask why, not to say one last thing. The urge feels urgent. It isn't. If you need to say it, say it somewhere it can't send, like our free [unsent text tool](/tools/unsent-text/).
- **Mute them everywhere.** Their stories and posts are fresh wounds every time you look.
- **Tell two people.** You don't need to explain it well. "We broke up, I'm not okay" is enough.
- **Eat something small and drink water.** Your body is in shock, too.
- **Don't decide anything big.** Not moving out, quitting or "getting back at them". Feelings in the first days are loud and short-lived.

## The First Month: Let It Out, Don't Act on It

Once the shock wears off, the grief arrives properly. This is the phase where most of the healing happens, and where most of the setbacks come from.

**Feel it, on purpose.** Give the sadness a slot, like twenty minutes in the evening. Cry, listen to the sad songs, look at the photos if you need to, and then stop. Pushing feelings away tends to make them come back stronger ([Wegner et al., 1987](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.53.1.5)).

**Write it out.** In a randomized study, students who wrote about their feelings after a breakup avoided the rise in tension, fatigue and physical symptoms that a comparison group had ([Lepore and Greenberg, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1080/08870440290025768)). An [unsent letter to your ex](/blog/articles/unsent-letter-to-ex/) is a good place to start.

**Remind yourself why it ended.** A broken heart edits memory into a highlight reel. In a lab study, thinking about an ex's negative sides was the strategy that actually reduced love feelings, even though it felt worse in the moment ([Langeslag and Sanchez, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000360)). Write five real reasons it didn't work. Our free [reasons not to text them](/tools/reasons-not-to-text/) tool turns them into a lock screen.

**Lean on people.** In a survey of more than 5,000 people after breakups, those who coped well used friends and family as a safe place, while people anxious about relationships more often turned to alcohol or drugs ([Davis et al., 2003](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167203252884)).

**Protect your nights.** Heartbreak is loudest at night, when there's nothing to distract you. Here's [why breakups hurt more at night](/blog/articles/why-breakups-hurt-more-at-night/) and what helps.

## Months Two and Beyond: Rebuild

As the sharp pain fades into a dull one, the work shifts from surviving to rebuilding.

- **Find yourself again.** After a breakup, people's sense of who they are tends to become less clear, and that confusion adds to the distress ([Slotter et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167209352250)). Pick up something you dropped for the relationship, or try something new.
- **Be gentle with yourself.** In a study of 109 divorcing adults, people who spoke about the split with more self-compassion had less intrusive distress up to nine months later ([Sbarra et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611429466)).
- **Expect anniversaries to hurt.** Birthdays, holidays and "a year ago today" will sting. A bad day at month four isn't a relapse. It's a reminder touching a bruise that's still healing.
- **Notice what you don't miss.** The arguments, the waiting for replies, the version of you that was always on edge.

Your heart doesn't need to be "fixed" in one go. It heals the way bones do: slowly, then all at once you realize it's been a while since it hurt.

## Is Broken Heart Syndrome Real?

Yes, but it's rare and it's different from everyday heartbreak. Broken heart syndrome, also called takotsubo or stress cardiomyopathy, is a heart condition usually brought on by a sudden, intense emotional or physical stress, such as a loss. It's thought to be triggered by a surge of stress hormones. It's more common in women and in people over 50 ([Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/broken-heart-syndrome/symptoms-causes/syc-20354617)), and most people recover fully within about a month ([Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/heart-health/is-broken-heart-syndrome-becoming-more-common)).

The symptoms, chest pain and shortness of breath, look like a heart attack. **If you have chest pain or trouble breathing, call emergency services.** Don't assume it's "just" heartbreak.

## When Heartbreak Is More Than Heartbreak

Sadness after a breakup is normal and can be intense. In a study that followed women for 30 weeks after a breakup, most were resilient or recovered quickly, some recovered slowly, and a small group stayed distressed. The slower groups ruminated more ([Verhallen et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/smi.3123)).

Talk to a doctor or therapist if, after a few weeks:

- You can't sleep, eat or work most days
- You feel hopeless or numb most of the time
- You're drinking or using to get through the day
- Nothing is getting even slightly easier

If you have any thoughts of harming yourself, contact a crisis line now. [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com) lists free, confidential lines for your country, and in the US you can call or text 988.

## FAQ: How to Heal a Broken Heart

**How long does it take to heal a broken heart?**
It's usually shorter than it feels. In one study, distress after a breakup fell steadily over ten weeks and was lower than people expected. Many people feel a real shift within one to three months. Our [breakup recovery timeline](/blog/articles/how-long-does-it-take-to-get-over-a-breakup/) goes week by week.

**Can you die of a broken heart?**
Everyday heartbreak isn't dangerous to your heart, but a rare condition called broken heart syndrome can follow intense stress. It's usually temporary and treatable. Chest pain or breathlessness always needs urgent medical help.

**Why does my chest hurt after a breakup?**
Emotional pain and physical pain share brain pathways, and stress hormones run high in the first weeks. A tight or heavy chest is common. If it's sharp, crushing or comes with breathlessness, get it checked right away.

**How do I heal a broken heart when I still love them?**
You don't have to stop loving them first. Love fades with distance and time. What you can change is how much you feed it: contact, scrolling and replaying the good memories. See [how to stop loving someone](/blog/articles/how-to-stop-loving-someone/).

**What should I not do with a broken heart?**
Don't text them, don't keep checking their profiles, don't drink to sleep, and don't make big decisions in the first weeks. Our [10 steps to get over a breakup](/blog/articles/how-to-get-over-a-breakup/) has a full "what not to do" table.

**Does a broken heart ever fully heal?**
For most people, yes. You may always remember them, and some dates may sting for a while. But the ache that fills every hour fades into a memory you can hold without it holding you.
