Telling your family can feel like a second breakup. You have to say it out loud, watch their faces, and answer questions you haven't answered for yourself yet.

It gets easier with a plan. Here's one.

## When Should You Tell Them?

When you're ready, and before they find out another way. A good sign you're ready: you can say "we broke up" without needing them to fix it.

If a family event is coming up, like a birthday or the holidays, tell the people you're close to beforehand. Finding out at the table is harder for everyone, including you. Our [holidays after a breakup](/blog/articles/holidays-after-a-breakup/) guide covers the day itself.

## Who Should You Tell First?

Start with the person who will take it best, usually the one you'd call in a crisis. Then ask them to help.

"Can you let Grandma and the cousins know? I don't want to explain it ten times." Most families have someone who's glad to do this.

## What to Say

Three parts are enough: what happened, how you are, and what you need.

| Who | What you can say |
| --- | --- |
| Parents | "Sam and I broke up. I'm sad, but I think it was right. I don't want to go over the details yet. I'd just like you to check in on me." |
| A sibling or close relative | "We broke up last week. It's been rough. Can I come over Sunday?" |
| Wider family | "We're not together anymore. I'm okay, thanks for asking." |
| Someone who pushes for details | "I'd rather not get into it. How's the new house?" |
| Someone who says "you'll get back together" | "We won't, and I'd like you to stop hoping we will." |

You don't owe anyone the reasons. "It wasn't working" is a complete answer.

## What If Your Family Loved Your Ex?

That's one of the hardest parts. They may be sad too, or even take it personally. Their feelings are real, but they're not yours to manage right now.

Be clear about what you need:

- **Ask them not to stay in touch with your ex**, at least for now, or not to tell you about it if they do. Updates from your mom count as contact by another route. See [what counts as breaking no contact](/blog/articles/what-counts-as-breaking-no-contact/).
- **Ask them not to pass messages on**, in either direction.
- **Ask them to keep your ex out of the conversation** for a while, including "I saw Sam at the store".

If there are children, the family relationship with your ex may need to continue. That's fine. Ask for it to stay about the kids.

## What If They Say the Wrong Thing?

They probably will. "There are plenty of fish in the sea." "I never liked her anyway." "At least you didn't get married."

Most of it is clumsy care. You can tell them what helps instead: "I know you mean well. What I need right now is for you to just listen." And if a relative keeps making it worse, it's okay to see less of them for a while.

## Getting Support Without Telling Everyone

You don't have to process this through your family. Friends, a therapist, writing it down or our free [unsent text tool](/tools/unsent-text/) all count. Some people find it easier to write out what they'd say to their ex first, so they're calmer when they talk to family. ## FAQ: Telling Family About a Breakup

**How do I tell my parents I broke up?**
Keep it short: that it ended, how you're feeling and what you need from them. You don't need to explain why. Telling them in person or on a call, before a family event, is usually easiest.

**Do I have to tell my family why we broke up?**
No. "It wasn't working" or "I'd rather not go into it" is enough. You can share more later if you want to.

**What if my family still talks to my ex?**
Ask them not to share updates with you or pass messages on. If they keep in touch for their own reasons, that's their choice, but you can ask them to keep it away from you.

**What if my family wants us to get back together?**
Tell them clearly that it's over and that hoping out loud makes it harder for you. Then change the subject. You may need to say it more than once.
