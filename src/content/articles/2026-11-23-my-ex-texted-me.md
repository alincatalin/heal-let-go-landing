You were doing okay. Maybe not great, but okay. Then your phone lights up with their name, and suddenly it's the only thing in the room.

"hey." Or "I saw this and thought of you." Or "I miss you."

Your thumb is already on the keyboard. Before it moves, read this. It takes five minutes, and the text will still be there.

## Should I Text My Ex Back?

**Not right now, and maybe not at all.** You don't owe your ex a reply, and you definitely don't owe them a fast one. What matters is what the message is actually asking for.

| What they sent | What it usually means | Reply? | If you reply |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "Do you still have my jacket?" (keys, bills, a pet, kids) | A practical need | Yes, about that only | "Yes. I'll leave it with Sam on Friday." |
| "hey" / "how are you?" | Testing whether the door is open | Not needed | Leave it, or "I'm okay, thanks." and nothing more |
| "I miss you" | They feel something right now. It doesn't mean anything has changed | Wait at least a day | "I'm not ready to talk. Please give me some space." |
| "Happy birthday" / "Merry Christmas" | A low-risk way to stay in your life | Optional | "Thanks." (a thank-you, not a question back) |
| "Saw this and thought of you" (a meme, a song) | Keeping a thread open without saying anything | Not needed | Leave it |
| A long apology | Guilt, closure, or a first step back | Wait, then decide | "Thank you for saying that. I'm still taking space." |
| "Can we talk?" | They want something: closure, comfort or a second try | Wait at least a day | "Not right now. If that changes, I'll reach out." |
| Threats, cruelty or guilt-tripping | Control | No | Don't reply. Save the messages. Block. |

The pattern: **answer needs, not feelings.** A practical message gets a practical answer. A feelings message is asking you to carry their feelings, and you're allowed to set that down.

## Why Did My Ex Text Me Out of Nowhere?

From one message, you can't know. That's the hard part, and it's why the text feels so loud. But most "out of nowhere" texts come from a short list of reasons:

- **They're lonely or bored.** A quiet night, a bad week, a breakup of their own. Reaching out to you is familiar and easy.
- **Something reminded them.** A song, a place, an anniversary, a holiday. The reminder is real, but it says more about their evening than about your future.
- **They want closure or forgiveness.** Some apologies are sincere. Some are mostly about making themselves feel better.
- **They're checking the door is still open.** Low-effort texts like "hey stranger" keep you on the hook without asking for anything. That's [breadcrumbing](/blog/articles/what-is-breadcrumbing/).
- **They want you back, for now.** If the message arrives right when you've started moving on, with big promises and no real change, read up on [hoovering](/blog/articles/what-is-hoovering/).
- **They actually miss you and have changed.** It happens. But real change shows up as consistent action over time, not one late-night text.

Notice that only the last reason is about a real second chance, and even that one doesn't need an answer tonight. If they've reappeared in other ways too, like story views or likes, our free [is my ex hoovering me quiz](/tools/is-my-ex-hoovering-me/) helps you sort out which one you're looking at.

## What to Do in the First Hour

The first hour is when you're most likely to send something you wouldn't send tomorrow. Use it for this instead:

1. **Don't reply yet.** There's no deadline. A fast reply mostly tells them you were waiting.
2. **Put the phone down for ten minutes.** Walk, shower, make tea. The first surge of feeling passes faster than it feels like it will.
3. **Tell one person.** A friend who knows the story can say what you'd say to them if it were their ex.
4. **Write your reply somewhere it can't send.** Get it all out: the angry version, the hopeful version, the "I miss you too". Our free [unsent text tool](/tools/unsent-text/) works for this, and so does Heal.
5. **Sleep on it.** Decide in daylight, not at 1am. Feelings are louder at night, and so are bad decisions about exes.

## Three Questions Before You Reply

When you've waited, ask yourself these. Be honest; nobody is watching.

**1. What do I want to happen after I hit send?**
If the answer is "I want them to say they miss me", "I want to know if they're seeing someone" or "I want us to get back together", the reply isn't really a reply. It's a request, and you're handing them the power to answer it.

**2. Could I handle any response back, including none?**
They might answer warmly, coldly, or not at all. They might reply and then go quiet for a week. If one of those outcomes would undo you, you're not ready to open the conversation.

**3. Would I still send this tomorrow morning?**
If yes, it's probably fine. If you're not sure, that's your answer for today.

## How to Reply to Your Ex (If You Decide To)

If you reply, keep it short, kind and closed. A closed reply answers without asking anything back, so it doesn't start a conversation you didn't choose.

**For something practical:**
> "Yes, I have it. I'll leave it with your sister on Saturday."

**To be polite without opening the door:**
> "Thanks for checking in. I'm doing okay."

**To ask for space:**
> "I'm not ready to talk right now. Please give me some space."

**To close it for good:**
> "I'm not going to get back together, and I'd like you to stop contacting me. Take care."

**If you're genuinely open to talking:**
> "I'm open to one conversation, in person, during the day. Not over text."

That last one is only for when you've thought about it for days, not minutes, and you know what would have to be different this time. Our guide on [what to text your ex](/blog/articles/what-to-text-your-ex/) has more examples for logistics, getting your things back and apologies.

What to leave out: questions ("how are you?", "are you okay?"), late-night replies, long explanations of how you've been, and anything you'd be embarrassed to see screenshotted.

## What Happens If I Don't Reply?

Usually, not much. People often expect silence to cause a scene, but most unanswered "hey" texts just stay unanswered.

A few things that can happen:

- **They don't text again.** That tells you how much weight the message carried.
- **They text again a few days or weeks later.** Same rules. A second "hey" isn't more urgent than the first.
- **They push harder** ("why are you ignoring me?"). Send one boundary text, like the "ask for space" one above, and then stop. If it keeps going, it's okay to [block your ex](/blog/articles/should-i-block-my-ex/).

Not replying isn't rude. After a breakup, it's a reasonable way to protect a decision you already made.

## Does Replying Break No Contact?

Yes, a reply is contact, even a short one. Not replying keeps your no contact intact, and so does a brief answer to a genuine practical need. Our guide on [what counts as breaking no contact](/blog/articles/what-counts-as-breaking-no-contact/) has the full list, including birthday texts and story views.

If you already replied, don't panic. One reply doesn't erase the days you've done. Read [I broke no contact, now what?](/blog/articles/i-broke-no-contact/) and pick it back up today. The [no contact rule guide](/blog/articles/no-contact-rule-guide/) covers how long to keep going and why.

## What the Research Says About Staying in Touch

It's tempting to think one friendly reply can't hurt. The research suggests contact makes it slower to feel better.

- In a study that followed 122 recently separated adults over five months, people who had more in-person contact with their ex reported more distress about the separation two months later. Contact slowed the expected recovery, and the effect showed up for people without children ([O'Hara et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/2167702620916454)).
- In a study of young adults in the month after a breakup, those who stayed in contact with their ex saw their sadness and love for the ex fade more slowly ([Sbarra and Emery, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1350-4126.2005.00112.x)).

Neither study says you must never answer a text. They say that ongoing contact keeps the breakup going, so each reply is worth deciding on purpose, not on impulse.

If your ex was abusive, threatening or controlling, their texts can be a safety issue. Don't reply, keep a record, tell someone you trust, and find a local helpline at [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com).

## FAQ: My Ex Texted Me

**Should I text my ex back after no contact?**
Only if it's about something practical. A reply to "hey" or "I miss you" ends no contact and usually restarts the hardest part of it. If you're mid no contact, the kindest thing for yourself is often to leave it.

**How long should I wait to reply to my ex?**
At least a day for anything emotional, and longer if you're still hurting. A practical message can get a practical answer when it suits you. There's no rule that says you must answer at all.

**What does it mean when your ex texts you after months?**
It means they thought of you, nothing more certain than that. Something reminded them, they're lonely, or they want to know if the door is open. If they want something real, they'll show it with consistent actions, not one message.

**Is it rude to ignore my ex's text?**
No. After a breakup, not replying is a boundary, not rudeness. If they keep messaging, one short "please give me some space" is enough.

**My ex texted me happy birthday or merry Christmas. Should I reply?**
You can say "Thanks" and leave it there, or not reply at all. Holiday and birthday texts are low-effort ways to stay in your life. See [should I text my ex happy birthday](/blog/articles/should-i-text-my-ex-happy-birthday/) for the other side of it.

**My ex texted "I miss you." What do I say?**
You don't have to say anything. If you want to answer, "I'm not ready to talk. Please give me some space" is honest and closes the loop. "I miss you" is a feeling, not a plan. Ask yourself what, if anything, has actually changed.

**Should I text my ex back if I want them back?**
Wait until you're sure you want them, not just the relief of hearing from them. Then suggest one conversation in person, and talk about the problem that ended it. If nothing has changed, the same ending is waiting. Our main guide, [should I text my ex?](/blog/articles/should-i-text-my-ex/), goes deeper on this.
