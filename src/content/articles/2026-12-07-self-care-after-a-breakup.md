After a breakup, self-care advice often sounds like a spa brochure. Light a candle. Take a bath. Treat yourself.

None of that is wrong. But when you haven't slept properly in a week and you can't remember your last real meal, what you need is simpler: getting the basics done while your head is somewhere else. Here's a realistic version.

## What Is Self-Care After a Breakup?

**Self-care after a breakup is looking after your body, mind and days on purpose, because the breakup is making it hard to do automatically.** Stress knocks out sleep, appetite and routine. Self-care is putting them back, one small thing at a time.

It isn't:

- **Pampering only.** Nice things help, but sleep and food come first.
- **Staying busy every minute.** Some space to feel it is part of healing.
- **Being "fine."** You can look after yourself and still be sad.

## Your Daily Breakup Self-Care Checklist

You don't need to tick everything. On a hard day, three is a win.

**Body**
- [ ] Woke up at roughly the usual time
- [ ] Ate at least two proper meals
- [ ] Drank water through the day
- [ ] Moved for 20 minutes (a walk counts)
- [ ] Got some daylight

**Mind**
- [ ] Let myself feel it for a few minutes, on purpose
- [ ] Wrote down one thought instead of replaying it
- [ ] Did one thing that took my full attention

**People**
- [ ] Talked to or texted one person who cares about me
- [ ] Said yes to one plan, or made one

**Phone**
- [ ] Didn't check their profile
- [ ] Didn't open our old chat
- [ ] Phone out of the bedroom at night

**Me**
- [ ] Did one small thing that's only mine

## Why Do the Basics Matter So Much?

**Because a breakup is a major stress, and stress hits the body first.** In a study of 192 students, high breakup distress was linked to sleep problems, intrusive thoughts and signs of depression and anxiety ([Field et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20432597/)). Looking after the body gives your mind a steadier base to heal from.

| Basic | Why it helps after a breakup | Easiest version |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sleep | Lack of sleep makes emotions louder and harder to manage | Same wake-up time every day |
| Food | Skipped meals make low mood and anxiety worse | Something simple, twice a day |
| Movement | Exercise reduces symptoms of depression | A 20-minute walk |
| Daylight | Helps your body clock and mood | Morning coffee outside |
| People | Isolation makes everything heavier | One text to one friend |

On movement: a large review of 218 trials found that walking or jogging, yoga, strength training and other exercise all reduced symptoms of depression ([Noetel et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-075847)). You don't need a gym. A walk counts.

On sleep: the NHS recommends going to bed only when sleepy, getting up at the same time every day, avoiding screens before bed and cutting caffeine and alcohol in the hours before sleep ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insomnia/)). If nights are the hardest part, read [can't sleep after a breakup](/blog/articles/cant-sleep-after-a-breakup/).

## Self-Care for Your Mind

- **Feel it in a set window.** Ten or twenty minutes to cry, write or listen to the sad songs. Then switch to something else. Fighting the feeling all day tends to make it louder.
- **Write it out.** In a study of students after a breakup, writing about it helped prevent the rise in stress symptoms that the comparison group had ([Lepore and Greenberg, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1080/08870440290025768)). Our [breakup journal prompts](/blog/articles/breakup-journal-prompts/) give you something to start with.
- **Give the urge to text somewhere to go.** Type it in our [unsent text tool](/tools/unsent-text/). It looks like their chat, and it never sends.
- **Be careful with what you feed your mind.** Sad playlists are fine in a window. Twelve hours of them isn't self-care.
- **Talk to yourself like a friend would.** "This is really hard, and I'm getting through it" beats "I should be over this by now."

## Self-Care for Your Phone

Your phone is where most breakup setbacks happen. Looking after yourself includes looking after what you see on it.

- **Mute or archive their chat** so it isn't the first thing you see.
- **Mute their stories and posts,** and those of friends who post them. Our guide on [how to stop checking their Instagram](/blog/articles/stop-checking-ex-instagram/) helps.
- **Move old photos** into a hidden album. You don't have to delete them.
- **Charge your phone outside the bedroom.** Late-night scrolling is where the worst messages get written.
- **Decide about blocking calmly.** Our guide on [whether to block your ex](/blog/articles/should-i-block-my-ex/) walks through it.

## Self-Care on Really Bad Days

Some days the checklist is too much. That's okay. On a really bad day, aim for the minimum:

1. Drink some water.
2. Eat one thing.
3. Tell one person "today is bad."
4. Get outside for five minutes.
5. Don't contact them today.

That's enough. Tomorrow you can do more.

If the bad days keep stacking up for weeks, you can't function, or you have thoughts of hurting yourself, please reach out now. In the US, call or text 988. Elsewhere, find a free helpline at [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com). Our guide to [depression after a breakup](/blog/articles/depression-after-a-breakup/) explains the signs.

## Self-Care Ideas That Rebuild You

Once the basics are steadier, add things that remind you who you are without them:

- A class or hobby you put off while you were together
- A new route for your walk, a new café, a new playlist
- A trip, even a day trip, somewhere you've never been with them
- Reconnecting with a friend you saw less of during the relationship
- Rearranging your room so it feels like yours again
- Learning to cook one meal you love

Breakups often blur your sense of who you are. Rebuilding it, one small thing at a time, is part of [getting over a breakup](/blog/articles/how-to-get-over-a-breakup/). ## FAQ: Self-Care After a Breakup

**What should I do for myself after a breakup?**
Start with the basics: regular sleep, real food, a daily walk and contact with one person you trust. Then add one small thing each day that's only yours. Keep your phone away from your ex's profile.

**Is it okay to stay in bed after a breakup?**
For a day or two, yes. After that, try to get up at a regular time, even if you don't do much. Staying in bed for days tends to make low mood worse.

**Is self-care selfish after a breakup?**
No. Looking after yourself is what lets you function, show up for others and heal. It's the opposite of selfish.

**What's the difference between self-care and distraction?**
Self-care meets a real need, like rest, food or connection. Distraction takes your mind off things. Both help, but distraction alone, like scrolling for hours, can leave you more tired and no further on.

**How do I practice self-love after a breakup?**
Treat yourself the way you'd treat a close friend going through it: with patience, practical help and kind words. Notice what you did well today, even if it's small.

**How long should I focus on self-care after a breakup?**
As long as you need. Most people find the basics get easier within a few weeks. Many keep the habits that helped, because they're good for life, not just breakups.
