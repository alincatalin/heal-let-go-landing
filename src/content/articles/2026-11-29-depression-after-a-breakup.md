After a breakup, it's normal to feel flattened. You might cry every day, stop eating, sleep all weekend or not at all, and wonder if you'll ever feel normal again.

Most of the time, that's grief, and it eases. Sometimes it turns into depression, which is a medical condition that deserves treatment. This guide helps you tell the difference and know what to do about either.

## Is It Normal to Be Depressed After a Breakup?

**Feeling depressed after a breakup is very common. Clinical depression is less common, but breakups can trigger it.** Breakups measurably affect mental health. In a US study of nearly 1,300 unmarried adults followed for 20 months, people's distress went up and life satisfaction went down after a breakup ([Rhoades et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023627)).

For most people, it's temporary. A study that tracked women for 30 weeks after a breakup found four paths: most were resilient or recovered fairly fast, some recovered slowly, and a small group (8 of 87) stayed distressed. The slower groups ruminated more ([Verhallen et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/smi.3123)). Relationship loss is also one of the life events researchers have studied as a risk factor for a first episode of depression in young people ([Monroe et al., 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-843X.108.4.606)).

## Heartbreak or Depression? How to Tell the Difference

| | Normal heartbreak | Possible depression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mood** | Waves of sadness, with some okay hours | Low, empty or numb most of the day, nearly every day |
| **Focus** | Mostly about the ex and the relationship | Spreads to everything: work, friends, yourself, the future |
| **Interest** | You can still enjoy some things, briefly | Nothing feels worth doing, even things you loved |
| **Self-view** | "I miss them", "I'm hurt" | "I'm worthless", "it's all my fault", "nothing will change" |
| **Body** | Poor sleep and appetite for a few weeks | Sleep, appetite and energy stay off for weeks |
| **Function** | Hard days, but you mostly get through them | You can't work, look after yourself or get out of bed |
| **Over time** | Slowly getting a little easier | Not easing, or getting worse |

The US National Institute of Mental Health describes depression as symptoms that last most of the day, nearly every day, for at least two weeks, including a low mood or loss of interest or pleasure ([NIMH](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression)).

## Signs of Depression After a Breakup

According to NIMH, common symptoms include ([NIMH](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression)):

- A sad, anxious or "empty" mood that won't lift
- Feeling hopeless, guilty or worthless
- Loss of interest or pleasure in things you used to enjoy
- Tiredness, low energy, moving or talking more slowly
- Trouble concentrating, remembering or making decisions
- Sleeping too much or too little
- Changes in appetite or weight
- Aches or pains without a clear physical cause
- Thoughts of death or suicide

Other warning signs: pulling away from everyone, drinking or using more to cope, or acting more impulsively.

If several of these have lasted two weeks or more, talk to a doctor. That's not an overreaction. Depression is treatable, and earlier help usually means faster recovery.

## What Helps With Depression After a Breakup

These help with heartbreak and with milder depression. They're not a replacement for treatment if you need it.

**1. Move your body, a little, every day.** In a review of 218 trials with over 14,000 people with depression, walking or jogging, yoga, strength training and mixed aerobic exercise all reduced depressive symptoms ([Noetel et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-075847)). A 20-minute walk counts.

**2. Keep a simple structure.** Get up at the same time, eat something at regular times, get outside in daylight. Depression feeds on empty, shapeless days.

**3. Stay in touch with people.** Even when you don't want to. One text to one friend a day. Isolation makes low mood worse.

**4. Cut the things that keep the wound open.** Contact with your ex and checking their profiles both keep distress going ([Sbarra and Emery, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1350-4126.2005.00112.x); [Marshall, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1089/cyber.2012.0125)). Mute them. See our [no contact rule guide](/blog/articles/no-contact-rule-guide/).

**5. Catch the rumination loop.** Going over and over what went wrong was linked to slower recovery in the 30-week study above. When you notice it, write the thought down once, then switch to something absorbing. Our guide on [how to stop thinking about your ex](/blog/articles/how-to-stop-thinking-about-your-ex/) has techniques.

**6. Talk to yourself like you'd talk to a friend.** In a study of divorcing adults, self-compassion while talking about the split predicted less intrusive distress months later ([Sbarra et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611429466)).

**7. Go easy on alcohol.** It's a depressant. It worsens sleep and next-day mood, and makes late-night texts more likely.

**8. Have someone to talk to at the worst hours.** Nights are often the lowest point. Here's [why breakups hurt more at night](/blog/articles/why-breakups-hurt-more-at-night/). ## When to Get Professional Help

Talk to a doctor, therapist or counsellor if:

- Symptoms have lasted two weeks or more and aren't easing
- You can't work, study or look after yourself
- You've stopped eating or sleeping properly for weeks
- You're drinking or using drugs to cope
- You feel hopeless, or that others would be better off without you

Treatments like talking therapy, medication or both work for most people ([NIMH](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression)). A GP or primary care doctor is a good first step. Many countries also have free mental health lines.

**If you have thoughts of suicide, don't wait.** Call or text 988 in the US, or find a line for your country at [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com).

## How Long Does Depression After a Breakup Last?

Heartbreak usually eases over weeks to a few months. In the 30-week study, most people were resilient or recovered fairly quickly ([Verhallen et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/smi.3123)). If you're still struggling to function after a month or two, or it's getting worse, that's a sign to get help, not to wait longer. ## FAQ: Depression After a Breakup

**Can a breakup cause depression?**
A breakup can trigger depression, especially in people who've had it before, who were blindsided, or who don't have much support. Most people feel very low for a while and recover. If symptoms last two weeks or more and affect daily life, see a doctor.

**What does severe depression after a breakup feel like?**
Not being able to get out of bed, eat or work, feeling numb or hopeless about everything, not just the relationship, and sometimes thoughts of not wanting to be alive. That needs professional help now.

**Is it normal to cry every day after a breakup?**
In the first weeks, yes. If it continues for many weeks with no easing, or comes with the other signs above, talk to someone.

**Why do I feel worse weeks after the breakup?**
The shock wears off and reality sets in. Many people find weeks two to six harder than the first days. That's common, but if it keeps getting worse, get support.

**Can I get over breakup depression on my own?**
Mild low mood often improves with time, exercise, sleep, people and no contact. Clinical depression usually needs treatment. There's no prize for doing it alone.

**Can an app help with depression after a breakup?**
Apps like Heal can help with the urges, the lonely nights and tracking progress. They're not a treatment for depression. If you think you're depressed, see a doctor as well.
