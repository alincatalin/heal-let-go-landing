You're fine at work. Fine at the gym. Then you're home, it's quiet, and she's all you can think about. You've opened her chat four times tonight. You haven't told anyone how bad it is.

That's more common than you'd think. Here's what actually helps you get over her, without pretending you're fine.

## Why Is It So Hard to Get Over Her?

**Because your brain is attached to her, and it treats losing her like withdrawal.** When researchers scanned people still in love after being rejected, looking at the ex lit up reward and craving areas linked to addiction ([Fisher et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00784.2009)). That's the pull you feel when you want to text her, check her stories or drive past her place.

There's also a pattern that's common for men after a breakup, though not universal:

- **You don't talk about it.** You tell friends "it's fine" and then sit with it alone at night.
- **You numb it.** More drinking, more gaming, more scrolling, more work. It works for a few hours, then it's back.
- **You lost your main confidant.** For many guys, she was the person they talked to about everything. Now that's gone too.

A large survey of 5,705 people in 96 countries found that women reported slightly more emotional and physical pain right after a breakup on average, but tended to recover more fully over time. The researchers suggested men often don't process the loss as fully and "simply move on" ([Morris, Reiber and Roman, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1037/ebs0000054)). Moving on without dealing with it can leave it hanging around for a long time.

## How Do You Get Over Her? 9 Steps That Work

1. **Go no contact.** No texts, no "happy birthday," no liking her posts. At least 30 days. Contact keeps the craving alive. Our [no contact rule guide](/blog/articles/no-contact-rule-guide/) explains how and why.
2. **Mute or block her everywhere.** If you can see her stories, you'll watch them. Decide with our guide on [whether to block your ex](/blog/articles/should-i-block-my-ex/).
3. **Tell one person the truth.** Not "I'm good, man." Something like "I'm not doing great, honestly." People with strong social connections tend to do better across the board: a large review of 148 studies found they had about 50% better odds of survival ([Holt-Lunstad, Smith and Layton, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316)).
4. **Write it down instead of sending it.** The 2am message to her never goes the way you hope. Type it in our [unsent text tool](/tools/unsent-text/), where it never sends.
5. **Move your body every day.** A run, the gym, a long walk. It burns off the restless energy and helps you sleep.
6. **Watch the alcohol.** Drinking lowers your guard and raises the odds of a drunk text. In a survey of over 5,000 people, more anxious coping after a breakup was linked to more drinking and drug use ([Davis, Shaver and Vernon, 2003](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167203252884)).
7. **Fill the evenings.** The hours you spent with her are the hardest now. Put something in them: friends, a sport, a class, a project.
8. **Make a list of what didn't work.** Your memory will replay the best moments. Balance them with the real ones. Our [reasons not to text your ex tool](/tools/reasons-not-to-text/) helps you write and keep the list.
9. **Track your days.** Watching the number grow is proof you're doing it. Use our free [no contact counter](/tools/no-contact-counter/).

## What Shouldn't You Do?

| Tempting move | Why it backfires | Do this instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Texting her "just to talk" | Restarts the craving and the waiting | Write it unsent, wait 24 hours |
| Checking her Instagram | Every check is a fresh hit of hurt | Mute her stories and posts |
| Drinking to forget | Numbs it for a night, then a drunk text | Plan sober evenings with friends |
| Jumping into a rebound | Often about her, not the new person | Wait until you're not comparing |
| Posting to make her jealous | It's a message to her in disguise | Post nothing for 30 days |
| Asking friends about her | Keeps her at the center of your day | Ask them not to pass on news |

## How Do You Stop Thinking About Her?

You can't force a thought out of your head. Trying not to think about something tends to make it come back stronger ([Wegner et al., 1987](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.53.1.5)).

What works better:

- **Notice it and redirect.** "There she is again." Then do something that needs your attention: a workout, a game with friends, cooking.
- **Give the thoughts a time slot.** Ten minutes in the evening to write about her, then close it.
- **Take away the triggers.** Photos on your phone, her hoodie, the playlist. Put them away for now. Our guide on [what to do with your ex's stuff](/blog/articles/what-to-do-with-your-exs-stuff/) helps.

## What If I Want Her Back?

That's normal, especially in the first weeks. The craving makes getting back together feel like the answer to everything.

Give it 30 days of real no contact before you decide anything. Then ask yourself whether you want her, or just want the pain to stop. Our post on [should I get back with my ex](/blog/articles/should-i-get-back-with-my-ex/) walks through it honestly. If she reaches out first, read [what to do when your ex texts you](/blog/articles/my-ex-texted-me/).

Wondering what's going on in her head during no contact? Honestly, you can't know. Focus on what you can control.

## How Long Does It Take to Get Over Her?

There's no fixed number. For most people the sharpest pain eases over weeks to a few months, with bad days along the way. It usually takes longer when you stay in contact or keep checking her profile. Our post on [how long it takes to get over a breakup](/blog/articles/how-long-does-it-take-to-get-over-a-breakup/) covers what research says.

Signs you're getting there:

- You go hours, then days, without checking her profile.
- A song or place reminds you of her and it stings less.
- You're making plans because you want to, not to stay busy.
- You can imagine her with someone else without it wrecking your day.

If you're struggling to sleep, eat or work for weeks, or you've had thoughts of hurting yourself, talk to someone now. In the US, call or text 988. Elsewhere, find a free helpline at [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com). Read [depression after a breakup](/blog/articles/depression-after-a-breakup/) for the signs.

## FAQ: How to Get Over Her

**How do I get over a girl I really loved?**
Give it time and distance. Cut contact, mute her online, and let yourself feel it instead of numbing it. Talk to someone you trust. The love doesn't switch off, but it fades when you stop feeding it.

**Why can't I stop thinking about my ex-girlfriend?**
Because your brain still craves the connection. Every check of her profile or reread of old messages keeps that craving going. Less contact, fewer reminders and a full schedule help the thoughts fade.

**Is it weak to be this upset over a girl?**
No. Breakups hurt everyone, and research shows rejection activates the same brain areas as physical pain. Dealing with it properly takes more strength than pretending you're fine.

**Should I text her to see how she's doing?**
Usually not. It reopens the wound and puts you back in the position of waiting for a reply. If you're unsure, take our [should I text my ex quiz](/tools/should-i-text-my-ex/) first.

**Will a rebound help me get over her?**
Sometimes it helps people feel wanted, but if you're mainly trying to forget her or make her jealous, it usually doesn't last. See [rebound relationships](/blog/articles/rebound-relationship/) for how to tell.

**How do I get over her if I see her every day?**
Keep things polite, short and about what's necessary. Don't linger or chat about personal things. Our guide to [no contact with a coworker](/blog/articles/no-contact-with-coworker/) has tips that work for classes and shared friend groups too.
