It's late. Everyone you could call is asleep. Your phone is in your hand, their name is one tap away, and the message is half written in your head.

You don't need a lecture right now. You need something to do with the next ten minutes. So here's a list, starting with the fastest.

## First: Give It Ten Minutes

You don't have to decide never to text them. You only have to decide not to text them in the next ten minutes.

That's a promise you can keep. Late-night urges tend to rise, peak and fall, and they usually feel very different once they've passed. If you still want to send it in the morning, take our 2-minute [should I text my ex? quiz](/tools/should-i-text-my-ex/) or read our guide on [whether to text your ex](/blog/articles/should-i-text-my-ex/) first, in daylight.

Now pick something from the list.

## In the Next Ten Minutes

### 1. Put the phone across the room

Plug it in somewhere you'd have to get up to reach. It sounds too simple to work, but most late-night texts are sent in bed, half asleep, with the phone already in your hand. A few steps of distance is a few seconds to change your mind.

### 2. Breathe slower than feels natural

Breathe in for four counts and out for six, for two minutes. A longer out-breath helps your body calm down, and a calmer body makes the urge easier to sit with. You don't need to feel better yet. You only need to get through the peak.

### 3. Write the text, but don't send it

Write exactly what you want to say, in full, somewhere it can't reach them: your notes app, a journal, or Heal's fake text, which never sends. Often the urge isn't about them reading it. It's about getting it out of your head.

Then read it back. Ask: what am I hoping they'll reply? What's more likely to happen? If they don't reply at all, how will I feel tomorrow?

### 4. Read your reasons

If you made a list of why the relationship ended, read it now. If you haven't, start one: three real reasons, written plainly. The version of you at 2am remembers the good parts. The list remembers the rest.

### 5. Name what you actually need

"I want to text my ex" is often shorthand for something else: I'm lonely, I can't sleep, I want to feel wanted, I want to know they're hurting too. Name it. Then ask whether a text to them would actually give you that. Usually it gives you a few seconds of relief and a long wait.

## If You Still Can't Sleep

### 6. Change rooms, change the light

Get up. Sit somewhere else, turn on a low light, get a glass of water. Lying in the dark replaying the relationship keeps the loop going. Moving breaks it a little.

### 7. Get it out on paper

Write about the breakup for ten minutes without stopping: what you miss, what you're angry about, what you're scared of. Don't edit it, and don't send it to anyone. You can throw it away after. The point is to give the thoughts somewhere to go that isn't their inbox.

### 8. Listen to something that isn't about them

A podcast, an audiobook, a long video on something you're curious about. Something with a voice in it helps more than silence. Skip the playlist you shared, and skip the sad songs for tonight.

### 9. Message someone else, or leave a message for the morning

If a friend is awake, text them instead. If no one is, write a message to a friend you'll send in the morning, or tell them tomorrow that last night was hard. The urge to reach out is real. Reach out to someone who's on your side.

### 10. Stay off their profiles

Checking their Instagram or their last active status feels like a smaller step than texting, but it feeds the same urge, and it's often what tips you into sending something. If you keep ending up there, our guide on [how to stop checking your ex's Instagram](/blog/articles/stop-checking-ex-instagram/) can help.

## Before Tomorrow Night

### 11. Plan for the next 2am

The urge tends to come back at the same time and in the same place. Decide now what you'll do next time: which step from this list, which friend, where the phone goes. Some people mute or archive the chat so their name isn't the first thing they see.

### 12. Count the night you didn't send it

If you got through tonight without texting, that counts. Write it down. Every night you ride the urge out, it gets a little easier, and the record helps on the nights when it doesn't feel that way.

## What If I Already Sent It?

It happens, and it doesn't undo everything. Don't follow it up with a second message or an apology for the first. Put the phone away, go back to your reasons, and start again from today. If you're doing [no contact](/blog/articles/no-contact-rule-guide/), a slip resets the clock, not your progress.

## FAQ: What to Do Instead of Texting Your Ex

**Why do I want to text my ex at night?**
Night is when you're tired, alone and not distracted, so the thoughts you kept busy with during the day catch up with you. Lower energy also makes it harder to hold back. The urge is common and it passes.

**How do I stop myself from texting my ex?**
Make sending harder and waiting easier. Put the phone out of reach, give yourself ten minutes before deciding, write the message somewhere it can't send, and read your reasons for the breakup. Plan ahead for the times the urge usually hits.

**Should I delete my ex's number?**
It can help if you know you'll text in a weak moment, though many people know the number by heart or can find it again. Muting or archiving the chat is a gentler option that still keeps their name off your screen.

**Is it bad to text my ex when I'm drunk or can't sleep?**
It's rarely a good idea. Messages sent late or after drinking tend to say more than you mean, and the reply, or the silence, lands when you're least able to handle it. If something really needs saying, it will still need saying in the morning.
