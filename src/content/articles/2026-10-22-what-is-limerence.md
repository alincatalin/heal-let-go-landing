If you've ever reread one text from him forty times trying to decode a full stop, you know the feeling already. Now it has a name.

## What Does Limerence Mean?

**Limerence** (noun, pronounced LIM-er-ence) is a state of intense, often involuntary romantic attachment to one person, usually someone who doesn't clearly return the feelings. It comes with constant thoughts about them and a mood that depends on their response. Someone in that state is **limerent**.

Merriam-Webster gives two senses: the euphoric feeling when you first fall for someone, and "a state of intense often involuntary romantic attachment to a person who does not reciprocate the feelings and that is often characterized by excessive preoccupation and obsessive behaviors" ([Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/limerence)). This page is about the second one, the kind that keeps you up at night.

## Where Does the Word "Limerence" Come From?

Tennov studied what being in love actually felt like and noticed the same pattern again and again: persistent, intrusive thoughts about one person, a racing heart just thinking about them, and agony when the feelings weren't returned. She coined a word for it in her 1979 book, *Love and Limerence* ([Tennov, 1979](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/limerence)).

She called the person at the center of it the "limerent object." Clinical, but a little comforting: enough people go through this that it needed its own term.

## What Are the Signs of Limerence?

You won't have every one of these, but the pattern is easy to spot.

- You think about them almost all the time, and not by choice.
- Everything they do seems to mean something. A like, a late reply, who they stood next to at the party.
- You see them as better than they are, with the flaws edited out.
- Your mood depends on them. One warm message and you're flying for a day; silence and you crash.
- Your body joins in: racing heart, flushed face, a jittery, weak feeling.
- Hope keeps it going. As Psychology Today puts it, "Uncertainty is necessary for limerence" ([Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/limerence)).

In one study of six people who'd been through it, their accounts included constant rumination, anxiety, depression and a feeling that their sense of self was coming apart. The researchers concluded that people were relating to two versions of the person at once, the real one and an idealized one ([Willmott and Bentley, 2015](https://sussex.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Exploring_the_lived-experience_of_limerence_a_journey_toward_authenticity/23397428)).

## What Causes Limerence?

There's no single cause, and it can happen to anyone. Tennov found that some people in her study weren't looking for love at all when it started ([Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/limerence)). What the descriptions share is a recipe:

1. **Attraction.** You admire someone and feel drawn to them.
2. **A hint that they might feel it too.** Tennov stressed that it starts with how you read events: you "see, or think you see the hint of possible reciprocity" ([Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/limerence)).
3. **Uncertainty.** You can't tell how they really feel. Mixed signals, slow replies and on-again, off-again contact keep it alive.

Some things seem to make people more prone to it. Researchers have linked limerence with anxiety, depression and an anxious attachment style, and with earlier experiences of abandonment or trauma ([Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/limerence)). Those are links, not proof that one causes the other.

## How Is Limerence Different From Love?

They overlap, and limerence is often how love starts. The difference is what each needs to keep going.

Love does fine with certainty. You know where you stand, you can see his annoying habits, and you still choose him.

Limerence needs doubt. Once you're sure, either way, it usually cools. And it's mostly about getting a response (will he text, does he feel it too), with much less room for who he actually is.

| | Limerence | Love |
|---|---|---|
| What keeps it going | Uncertainty and hope | Knowing where you stand |
| What it focuses on | Getting a response from them | Who they actually are |
| Their flaws | Edited out | Seen, and you choose them anyway |
| Once you get a clear answer | Usually cools, either way | Holds up |
| How it feels day to day | Highs and crashes | Mostly calm |

A quick check: if he texted you right now, would you feel calm or high? Calm leans toward love.

## Why Does Limerence Make a Breakup Feel Unbearable?

Your brain keeps wanting him whether or not he wants you back.

In a brain-scan study of 15 people who had just been rejected but were still intensely in love, seeing a photo of the ex lit up reward and craving regions, including ones tied to addiction. The authors suggest the brain's reward system is involved in romantic passion whether you're happily or unhappily in love ([Fisher et al., 2010](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/jn.00784.2009)).

Put limerence on top of that and a breakup becomes one long wait for a sign. A story view feels like a message. A breakup hands limerence exactly the uncertainty it feeds on, so it can feel worse after the ending than it did during the relationship.

## How Does Limerence Fade?

Tennov described three typical endings ([Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/limerence)):

1. **Consummation.** The feeling is returned, you get together, and the intensity settles. Sometimes that turns into love. Sometimes the spell just breaks.
2. **Starvation.** Hope runs out. Nothing left to decode. It hurts, then it fades.
3. **Transference.** The feelings move to someone new and it starts again.

After a breakup, starvation is the one you get some say in. That means taking away the fuel: no texts, no checking his profile, no asking friends what he's up to. It's why [no contact](/blog/articles/no-contact-rule-guide/) helps so much with limerence, and why [checking his Instagram](/blog/articles/stop-checking-ex-instagram/) keeps it alive.

It also helps to argue with the idealized version: write down three real things that didn't work, and read them when the highlight reel starts.

### Signs Limerence Is Fading

As it wears off, the idealized picture of them fades first, and the intensity follows ([Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/limerence)). In practice that looks like:

- You notice their flaws without rushing to explain them away.
- A reply from them, or no reply, doesn't decide your whole day.
- You go hours without replaying a conversation.
- You stop checking their profile, sometimes without deciding to.
- You can imagine a future that doesn't include them, and it doesn't feel empty.

If you've been doing no contact, these overlap with the [signs no contact is working](/blog/articles/signs-no-contact-is-working/).

## FAQ: What Is Limerence?

**Is limerence the same as being in love?**
Not quite. Limerence is an involuntary longing that depends on uncertainty. Love can grow out of it, but love is calmer and holds up once you know where you stand.

**How long does limerence last?**
It can last a few weeks or many years, but most estimates put the average between one and three years ([Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/limerence)). It fades faster once there's nothing left to hope for or decode, which is why cutting contact helps.

**What does limerence feel like?**
Like you can't get them out of your head. Your heart races when their name appears, you reread their messages for hidden meaning, and your mood swings between high and crushed depending on what they do. Many people say it feels a lot like anxiety.

**Can you have limerence for an ex?**
Yes. A breakup adds the uncertainty it feeds on, so it often gets stronger after the ending.

**How do I stop limerence after a breakup?**
Take away what feeds it. Stop texting, stop checking their profile and stop asking friends about them, so there's nothing left to decode. Writing down a few real things that didn't work helps when your mind replays only the good parts.

**What is a limerent object?**
It's Tennov's term for the person you feel limerence for, the one your thoughts and moods revolve around.

**Is limerence a mental illness?**
No. It isn't in the DSM, the manual used to diagnose mental disorders, and Tennov saw it as something healthy people go through. Some experts argue it should count as a problem when it stops you functioning day to day ([Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/limerence)). If it's taking over your sleep or work, a therapist can help.
