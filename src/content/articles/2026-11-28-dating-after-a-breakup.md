Friends are saying "you should get back out there". The apps are a tap away. Part of you wants to feel wanted again. Part of you wants to hide until spring.

Both are normal. The question isn't whether there's a correct number of weeks. It's whether you're dating toward someone new or away from the last one.

## How Long Should You Wait to Date After a Breakup?

**There's no rule, and the "wait half the length of the relationship" idea has no research behind it.** What matters is where you are, not the calendar.

Studies on rebound relationships suggest starting something new soon isn't automatically harmful. In one series of studies, a shorter gap before a new relationship was linked to greater well-being and self-esteem ([Brumbaugh and Fraley, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407514525086)). But that doesn't mean faster is better for you. People who date mainly to avoid the pain often find it waiting for them a few weeks in.

A rough guide many people find useful:

| Where you are | What dating will probably feel like | Suggestion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Still in contact with your ex, or checking their profiles | Every date gets compared, and your ex is in the room | Focus on no contact first |
| First few weeks, still raw | Relief at first, then a crash | Wait. See friends instead |
| Thinking of them daily, but less | Possible, but expect waves of missing them | Low-pressure dates, no big commitments |
| Can go days without thinking of them | Curious and present | You're probably ready |
| You'd say no if they asked you back | Dating for the right reasons | Go for it |

## 10 Signs You're Ready to Date Again

Answer honestly. Most people won't tick all ten, but seven or more is a good sign.

1. You can go several days without thinking about your ex.
2. You've stopped checking their social media.
3. If your ex texted tonight, you wouldn't drop everything.
4. You want to meet someone, not just anyone.
5. You can talk about the breakup without crying or getting angry.
6. You know, at least roughly, what went wrong and what you want differently.
7. You're okay spending a Saturday night alone, even if you'd rather not.
8. You're not hoping your ex sees you dating.
9. You feel like yourself again, most days.
10. You can imagine a first date going badly and being fine.

If you're under five, that's not a failure. It means your energy is better spent on healing right now. Our [breakup recovery timeline](/blog/articles/how-long-does-it-take-to-get-over-a-breakup/) shows what usually comes next.

## Signs You're Not Ready Yet

- You're redownloading apps after a hard night
- You find yourself telling matches about your ex
- You feel worse after a good date, not better
- You're looking for someone who's the opposite of your ex, or exactly like them
- You still hope they'll come back. If that's you, read [will my ex come back?](/blog/articles/will-my-ex-come-back/) first

Dating while you're still deep in it can turn into a [rebound relationship](/blog/articles/rebound-relationship/). That's not always bad, but it's better to know you're doing it.

## Why Healing First Makes Dating Better

After a breakup, people's sense of who they are tends to become less clear, and that confusion adds to the distress ([Slotter et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167209352250)). When you date from that place, you're more likely to shape yourself around the new person, or pick someone who fills the gap your ex left rather than someone who fits you.

Taking time to rebuild your own routines, friendships and interests means that when you meet someone, there's a "you" for them to meet. In a study that followed young adults after a breakup, reflecting on the breakup over several weeks rebuilt people's sense of self and reduced loneliness ([Larson and Sbarra, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550614563085)).

## How to Start Dating Again

**Start small.** Coffee or a walk, not a dinner and a show. Low stakes, easy exits.

**Keep it light at first.** You don't need to find "the one" on date one. You're practicing being curious about someone new.

**Be honest without oversharing.** You don't need to tell the full story. "I came out of a relationship a few months ago and I'm taking things slowly" is honest and enough.

**Notice comparisons, don't act on them.** You'll catch yourself thinking "my ex would have..." That's normal. If it happens constantly, give it more time.

**Watch how you feel after, not just during.** A good sign: you feel lighter, or curious. A warning sign: you feel emptier, or rush to check your ex's profile.

**Pace it.** No rush to define it, meet families or post it.

## What to Say About Your Ex on a First Date

Keep it short and neutral, then move on.

> "I was in a relationship for a few years. It ended earlier this year. I'm doing well, and I'm glad to be meeting new people."

Avoid: how they hurt you, how they've moved on, what went wrong in detail, and anything you'd say more than once. If you find you can't stop talking about them, that's useful information about your readiness.

## Dating After a Long Relationship

After years with one person, dating can feel like a foreign country: new apps, new rules, and a version of yourself you haven't been in a while. Give yourself more time to rebuild before you start. Our guide to [getting over a long-term relationship](/blog/articles/how-to-get-over-a-long-term-relationship/) covers the identity side, which is usually the hardest part.

## FAQ: Dating After a Breakup

**How long after a breakup should you wait to date?**
There's no fixed rule. Many people feel ready within a few months, but the signs above matter more than the calendar. If you'd go back to your ex tomorrow, wait.

**Is it okay to date right after a breakup?**
It can be, if you're honest with yourself and the other person. Research doesn't show quick new relationships are always harmful. They go wrong when they're a way to avoid grief or make your ex jealous.

**How do I know if I'm over my ex enough to date?**
You can go days without thinking about them, you've stopped checking their profiles, and you wouldn't go back if they asked. If you're still waiting for them, focus on that first.

**Should I tell my date about my breakup?**
Briefly, if it comes up. One or two sentences is enough. Save the full story for later, if the relationship gets there.

**Why do I feel guilty dating after a breakup?**
Often because part of you is still attached, or worries what your ex would think. Guilt isn't a sign you're doing something wrong. If it's strong, talk it through with a friend or a coach.

**What if I meet someone great too soon?**
Go slowly. Be honest about where you are, and keep healing in parallel. Good people tend to respect "I'm taking it slow".
