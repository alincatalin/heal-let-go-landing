A week after the breakup you redownloaded the apps. Or someone new is already texting you good morning. Or your ex is posting with someone they met a month ago.

Is it a rebound? Is that bad? Does it ever last? The answers are more interesting than the usual "rebounds never work".

## What Is a Rebound Relationship?

**A rebound relationship is one that starts soon after a breakup, usually before the feelings from the last one have settled.** There's no official cutoff, but most people use the word for something that begins within weeks or a few months, while you're still hurting or thinking a lot about your ex.

The word carries a warning, but a rebound isn't automatically a mistake. What matters is **why** you're in it.

| Healthy new start | Rebound that tends to hurt |
| --- | --- |
| You like this person for who they are | Anyone would do, as long as you're not alone |
| You think about your ex less when you're with them | You compare them to your ex constantly |
| You're honest about where you are | You hide that you're not over it |
| You'd still want them if your ex came back | You'd drop them if your ex texted |
| You're not posting it for your ex to see | Part of the point is your ex seeing it |
| You can be alone, you just don't want to be | Being alone feels unbearable |

## What Does Research Say About Rebounds?

Less than you'd expect, and the findings push back on the stereotype.

- **Rebounds can help you let go.** In a series of studies, people who started a new relationship sooner after a breakup reported getting over their ex more and feeling more confident about their desirability. A shorter gap before the new relationship was linked to greater well-being and self-esteem ([Brumbaugh and Fraley, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407514525086), as summarized in [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/meet-catch-and-keep/201405/can-rebound-relationship-be-the-real-deal)).
- **Hope of someone new eases attachment for anxious people.** In research at the University of Toronto, anxiously attached people who were led to believe a new partner would be easy to find expressed less longing for their ex ([Spielmann et al., 2009](https://magazine.utoronto.ca/?p=12761)).
- **Men may rebound more, partly for lack of support.** In a study of 201 students, men were more likely to enter rebound relationships, linked to having less social support and more emotional attachment to an ex ([Shimek and Bello, 2014](https://doi.org/10.3390/socsci3010024)).

The pattern across these: a new connection can help, but rebounding to avoid feeling the breakup, or because you have no one else to lean on, is where it tends to go wrong.

## Signs You're in a Rebound Relationship

- It started within weeks of a breakup, and you haven't really grieved
- You talk about your ex a lot, or avoid them as a topic completely
- You check whether your ex has seen photos of you two
- The new relationship moved fast: "I love you", meeting family, moving in
- You feel more relief than excitement
- You compare them to your ex, in either direction
- You'd consider going back if your ex reached out
- When you're alone, the breakup still hits hard

Two or three of these doesn't doom it. It means you might be using the new person to carry some of the weight of the old breakup.

## Signs You're the Rebound

If you're dating someone who just came out of a relationship:

- Their ex comes up often, with anger or longing
- They're still in touch with their ex, or checking their profiles
- Things are intense, then they pull back without explanation
- They avoid labels or talk about "not being ready"
- They seem to need you more than know you

Ask directly: "How are you doing with the breakup?" Their answer, and how they handle the question, tells you a lot.

## The Stages of a Rebound Relationship

These aren't a scientific model. They're a pattern many people describe, and they're useful for spotting where you are:

1. **Relief.** Someone wants you. The ache eases. It feels like proof you're okay.
2. **Intensity.** Fast closeness, lots of time together, big feelings.
3. **Comparison.** The new person does something your ex did, or didn't, and old feelings surface.
4. **Reality.** The breakup you skipped catches up: sadness, doubt, missing your ex.
5. **Decision.** Either you choose this person for who they are, or it ends, often with a second round of grief for both breakups.

If you're in stage 3 or 4, it doesn't have to end. It means it's time to deal with the old breakup honestly, ideally with distance from your ex.

## Do Rebound Relationships Last?

Some do. There's no good data on average length, and the research above suggests rebounds aren't doomed by timing alone. What predicts whether it lasts is the same as for any relationship: compatibility, communication, and whether both of you are actually present in it.

The rebounds that tend not to last are the ones that were really about the ex: a distraction, a revenge, a bandage. Once the pain fades, the reason for the relationship goes with it.

## Should You Date After a Breakup?

There's no rule that says you must wait three months or six months. Ask yourself:

- **Do I want this person, or anyone?**
- **Could I go a week without thinking about my ex?**
- **If my ex texted tonight, what would I do?**
- **Am I okay being alone, even if I don't love it?**

If the honest answers point to "I want someone so I don't have to feel this", give it more time. ## If Your Ex Is the One Rebounding

Seeing your ex with someone new, fast, hurts in a specific way. It feels like proof you were easy to replace. Usually it's the opposite: they're avoiding the feelings you're sitting with.

You don't need to watch it happen. Mute them, ask friends not to pass on updates, and resist the urge to check. ## FAQ: Rebound Relationships

**How long do rebound relationships last?**
There's no reliable average. Some last weeks, some turn into long relationships. The ones built mainly on avoiding the breakup tend to end once that pain fades.

**Is it bad to rebound after a breakup?**
Not necessarily. Research links rebounds to feeling more confident and less attached to the ex. It becomes a problem when you're using someone to avoid grieving, or when you're not honest with them about where you are.

**Am I a rebound?**
Look for constant talk about their ex, ongoing contact with them, fast intensity and then pulling back, and avoiding labels. If you're not sure, ask how they're doing with the breakup and watch how they answer.

**Why did my ex move on so fast?**
Often because starting something new is easier than feeling the loss. A quick new relationship says more about how they cope than about how much you meant. See [why do I miss my ex](/blog/articles/why-do-i-miss-my-ex/) for your side of it.

**Can a rebound turn into a real relationship?**
Yes. It helps to slow down, talk honestly about the last breakup, and make sure you're choosing the person, not the relief.

**How do I stop comparing my new partner to my ex?**
Notice the comparisons without acting on them, and ask what they're telling you. Constant comparison is often a sign you need more time to process the old relationship first. Our [10 steps to get over a breakup](/blog/articles/how-to-get-over-a-breakup/) can help.
