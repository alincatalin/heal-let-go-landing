You've been going back and forth for days. Some hours you're sure it's over. Then a memory hits and you're sure you made a mistake. Maybe they've hinted they want to try again. Maybe you're the one thinking about it.

You don't need someone to tell you yes or no. You need better questions. Here they are.

## Should You Get Back With Your Ex?

**Get back together only if the problem that ended it has changed, and you can both show it, not just say it.** Missing someone is a feeling. Getting back together is a decision, and it needs more than a feeling to go well.

Couples do get back together often. In a study of young adults aged 17 to 24, nearly half had broken up and gotten back together with a partner at some point ([Halpern-Meekin et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0743558412464524)). Some of those second tries last. But on-again, off-again couples tend to report lower love and satisfaction, more uncertainty and more conflict than couples who never split ([Dailey, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108567756.003)).

So the question isn't "do people get back together?" They do. It's "will it be different this time?"

| Good reasons to try again | Reasons to wait or walk away |
| --- | --- |
| The problem that ended it was specific, and it has really changed | You mostly miss the comfort, the routine or being in a couple |
| You both can name what went wrong without blaming only the other | One of you still says the breakup was "nobody's fault" or "all your fault" |
| Time has passed, months not days, and you still want this person, not just relief | It's been a few weeks and the pain is still raw |
| They've changed in actions over time, not only in words | They promise change but nothing is different yet |
| You'd be okay if it didn't work out | You'd be devastated to lose them twice, or are scared of being alone |
| You feel safe with them | There was cheating that wasn't owned, control, threats or abuse |

## 10 Questions to Ask Yourself First

Take your time with these. Write the answers down. Our free [should I text my ex quiz](/tools/should-i-text-my-ex/) walks you through a shorter version in two minutes.

**1. Why did it end, really?**
Not the last argument. The reason underneath it. Different values, different plans, trust, how you fought, one person doing all the work. If you can't name it, you can't fix it.

**2. Has that thing changed?**
Something concrete, not "we've both grown". A new job that ended the long distance, therapy for the anger, a decision about kids. If nothing has changed, the same ending is waiting.

**3. Do I miss them, or do I miss having someone?**
Loneliness after a breakup is loud, especially at night and on holidays. Try this: if someone equally kind came along tomorrow, would you still want your ex? If not, it's the role you miss.

**4. Am I remembering the whole relationship?**
After a breakup, memory becomes a highlight reel. Write down three moments that hurt as clearly as three that were wonderful.

**5. How have they acted since the breakup?**
Consistent and respectful? Or hot and cold, texting at midnight and going quiet for a week? The way they behave now is a preview. If they keep reappearing with low-effort messages, read [what is breadcrumbing](/blog/articles/what-is-breadcrumbing/) and [what is hoovering](/blog/articles/what-is-hoovering/).

**6. Who wants it more?**
If you're the one pushing, persuading and waiting, getting back together usually means more of that.

**7. What would we do differently on day one?**
Not "try harder". Specific changes: how you'll handle the next fight, who moves where, how much time you spend together.

**8. What do my friends think?**
Not because they get a vote. Because they saw you during the relationship and after it. If the people who love you look worried, ask them why.

**9. Am I deciding from a calm place?**
Decisions made at 1am, after a few drinks, on their birthday or after seeing them with someone else are not decisions. They're reactions.

**10. Could I handle it ending again?**
On-again, off-again relationships can be draining. If going through another breakup would flatten you, that's a reason to be very sure before you start again.

If you answered most of these honestly and still think it could work, see the "if you decide to try" section below. If not, the answer is already in your notes.

## What the Research Says About Getting Back Together

There's more research on this than you'd think, and it's mixed.

- **It's common.** Nearly half of young adults in one large US study had broken up and gotten back together at least once ([Halpern-Meekin et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0743558412464524)). Researchers at Kansas State estimated that about 40% of college-age people were in a relationship that had broken up and gotten back together ([Kansas State University, 2012](https://www.k-state.edu/news/newsreleases/feb12/cyclicalrelationships22012.html)).
- **Lingering feelings are the main pull.** In research on why on-off partners reunite, continued feelings and attachment were the most common reasons ([Dailey et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1080/00224545.2010.503249)). Feelings bring people back. They don't fix what ended it.
- **Cycling has costs.** On-off couples report less love and satisfaction, more uncertainty and more conflict ([Dailey, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108567756.003)). In the Kansas State research, couples who broke up and reunited before marriage reported lower satisfaction and more trial separations in the first years of marriage ([Kansas State University, 2012](https://www.k-state.edu/news/newsreleases/feb12/cyclicalrelationships22012.html)).

None of this says a second chance can't work. It says the default outcome of getting back together without changing anything is more of the same.

## Signs It Could Work This Time

- You both had real time apart and used it to think, not just to wait.
- You can talk about the breakup calmly, without blaming only one person.
- The specific problem has a specific fix, and it's already in motion.
- They've shown change for a while, not just on the day they asked you back.
- Your friends and family aren't worried.
- You're choosing them, not running from being alone.

## Signs You Shouldn't Get Back Together

- The breakup involved cheating that was never owned, or lies about it.
- They were controlling, threatening, cruel or violent. If that's the case, it isn't a relationship decision, it's a safety one. Talk to someone you trust and find a local helpline at [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com).
- You've broken up and gotten back together several times for the same reason.
- You feel anxious, small or on edge around them.
- They want you back right when you've started moving on, with big promises and nothing behind them.
- You're mostly scared of being alone. If the relationship had a cycle of hurt and making up, read [what is a trauma bond](/blog/articles/what-is-a-trauma-bond/).

## If You Decide to Try Again

1. **Wait until the feelings have settled.** Give it weeks of no contact first, ideally longer. Our [no contact rule guide](/blog/articles/no-contact-rule-guide/) explains why distance helps you see clearly.
2. **Talk in person, during the day.** Not over text, not late at night.
3. **Name the problem out loud, together.** "We broke up because ___. This time, ___ is different."
4. **Go slowly.** Don't move back in or tell everyone straight away.
5. **Agree on a check-in.** In three months, ask honestly: is it actually different?
6. **Consider couples counselling.** Especially if the issue was communication, trust or conflict.

## FAQ: Should I Get Back With My Ex?

**Is it a good idea to get back with your ex?**
It can be, when the reason you split has really changed and you both show it over time. Without that change, on-again, off-again relationships tend to bring more uncertainty and conflict, not less.

**How long should you wait before getting back with an ex?**
Long enough that you're not deciding from fresh pain or loneliness. For most people that's months, not weeks. If you're still in the first month, wait.

**Do relationships work the second time around?**
Some do. The ones that work usually had a clear, fixable problem and time apart to fix it. If you'd be getting back together with nothing changed, expect the same ending.

**Why do I want to get back with my ex?**
Often it's lingering love, loneliness or the familiarity of them. Those are real feelings, but they don't tell you whether the relationship would work. Read [why do I miss my ex](/blog/articles/why-do-i-miss-my-ex/) to untangle them.

**My ex wants to get back together. What should I do?**
Don't answer the same day. Ask yourself the ten questions above, then ask them what's different now. If they can't say, or the answer is only "I miss you", that's your answer. If they texted out of the blue, see [my ex texted me](/blog/articles/my-ex-texted-me/).

**Should I get back with my ex if they cheated?**
Only if they fully owned it, without blaming you, and are willing to rebuild trust over a long time, usually with help from a counsellor. If you'd spend the relationship checking their phone, it won't feel like a relationship.
