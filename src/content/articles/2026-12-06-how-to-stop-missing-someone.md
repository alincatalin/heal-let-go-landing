Missing someone can feel physical. A tight chest when you see something they'd like. A reflex to tell them about your day, then remembering you can't. A quiet evening that feels too quiet.

You can't switch the feeling off. But you can make it smaller, less often and easier to carry. Here's how.

## Why Do We Miss People So Much?

**Because your brain and your routine were built around them.** Missing someone is your mind noticing that something important is gone and looking for it.

A few things are going on at once:

- **Attachment.** Being close to someone shapes how you calm down and feel safe. When they're gone, part of that system keeps reaching for them.
- **Craving.** Brain scans of people still in love after a breakup show activity in reward and craving areas linked to addiction ([Fisher et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00784.2009)). Missing is, in part, a craving.
- **Habit.** The morning text, the evening call, the person you told everything. Every habit that included them now has a hole in it.
- **Selective memory.** Your mind replays the best moments far more than the ordinary or painful ones.

If it's an ex you miss, our post on [why you miss your ex](/blog/articles/why-do-i-miss-my-ex/) goes deeper on each of these.

## How Do You Stop Missing Someone? 10 Things That Help

1. **Cut the reminders you control.** Mute their profiles, archive the chat, move photos to a hidden album. Every reminder is a small reopening. If you're stuck checking, read [how to stop checking their Instagram](/blog/articles/stop-checking-ex-instagram/).
2. **Stop the contact that keeps it fresh.** Each message restarts the missing. In a study of young adults after a breakup, love and sadness for the ex faded more slowly in those who stayed in contact ([Sbarra and Emery, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1350-4126.2005.00112.x)). A stretch of [no contact](/blog/articles/no-contact-rule-guide/) gives the feeling room to fade.
3. **Feel it in short doses.** Fighting the feeling tends to make it stronger. Give it ten minutes: cry, write, listen to the song. Then do something else.
4. **Say it somewhere safe.** "I miss you" wants to go somewhere. Write it in our free [unsent text tool](/tools/unsent-text/), a chat that looks like theirs but never sends.
5. **Remember the whole picture.** List the things you don't miss: the arguments, the waiting, the parts of you that got smaller. In one study, focusing on an ex's negative sides reduced feelings of love, even though it lowered mood for a while ([Langeslag and Sanchez, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000360)). Our [reasons not to text your ex tool](/tools/reasons-not-to-text/) helps you keep the list.
6. **Distract on purpose.** In the same research, distraction lifted mood. Pick things that take your full attention: a game with friends, exercise, a film you've wanted to see, cooking something new.
7. **Fill the specific gaps.** Who do you tell about your day now? What do you do on Sunday mornings? Give each empty slot a new person or plan.
8. **Lean on your people.** Missing someone feels heavier alone. Social connection matters a lot: a review of 148 studies found people with stronger social ties had about 50% higher odds of survival over the study periods ([Holt-Lunstad, Smith and Layton, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316)).
9. **Do something that's only yours.** A class, a trip, a new routine. It reminds you that you existed before them and will after.
10. **Count the days.** Watching the number grow is proof you can live without reaching out. Use our [no contact counter](/tools/no-contact-counter/).

## What to Do When Missing Them Hits Hard

The waves come without warning: a song, a smell, the time of day you used to talk. When one hits:

| In the moment | Instead of | Try |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You want to text them | Opening their chat | Writing it unsent, waiting an hour |
| You want to look at photos | Scrolling the whole album | Looking at your "what I don't miss" list |
| You want to check their profile | Searching their name | Texting a friend instead |
| You feel the ache physically | Lying in bed with it | A walk, a shower, slow breathing |
| It's late at night | Staying up replaying | Phone across the room, lights out |

Nights are often worst. Our post on [why breakups hurt more at night](/blog/articles/why-breakups-hurt-more-at-night/) explains why.

## How Do You Stop Missing Someone Who Doesn't Miss You?

This is one of the hardest kinds. It can feel like you're carrying the whole relationship alone.

- **You can't know what they feel.** People who seem fine are sometimes hurting. People who seem upset sometimes aren't. Guessing keeps you stuck.
- **Their feelings don't decide yours.** You're allowed to miss them and still choose to move on.
- **Missing isn't proof you should be together.** It's proof you cared. Both can be true.

## Is It Missing Them, or Missing How It Felt?

Often it's both. Sometimes it's mostly the second. Ask yourself:

- Do I miss them, or having someone?
- Do I miss who they were, or who they were at the start?
- Do I miss them, or who I was with them?

The answers can show you what you actually need now: company, affection, fun, or a sense of who you are. Many of those can come from other places while you heal. Our guide on [how to stop loving someone](/blog/articles/how-to-stop-loving-someone/) helps when the feelings go deeper than missing.

## How Long Does It Take to Stop Missing Someone?

There's no fixed time. For many people the sharp missing eases over weeks to a few months, with waves after that, often around dates and holidays. It usually takes longer if you're still in contact or the relationship was long. Our post on [how long it takes to get over a breakup](/blog/articles/how-long-does-it-take-to-get-over-a-breakup/) covers what research suggests.

What changes first isn't whether you miss them. It's how often, how strongly and how fast it passes. That's the sign you're healing. If you're in no contact, see what usually shifts by [3 months of no contact](/blog/articles/3-months-no-contact/).

## FAQ: Missing Someone

**Is it normal to miss someone every day?**
Yes, especially in the first weeks after they leave your life. Over time it usually becomes less frequent and less intense. If it stays overwhelming for months and you can't function, talk to a doctor or therapist.

**Why do I miss someone who hurt me?**
Because missing isn't a judgment of whether they were good for you. Your brain remembers comfort and good moments, and attachment doesn't check the facts. It helps to write down what actually happened.

**Should I tell them I miss them?**
Usually not, especially after a breakup. It often reopens things and leaves you waiting for a reply. Write it unsent first. If you still want to say it after a week, take our [should I text my ex quiz](/tools/should-i-text-my-ex/).

**How do I stop missing someone I see every day?**
Keep interactions short and about what's necessary, and build distance where you can, like sitting elsewhere or muting them online. See [no contact with a coworker](/blog/articles/no-contact-with-coworker/) for ideas that work in classes and friend groups too.

**Does missing someone mean I still love them?**
Not necessarily. You can miss someone out of habit, loneliness or memories without wanting them back. Missing fades faster than people expect once the contact and reminders stop.

**How do I stop missing someone at night?**
Plan your evenings, keep your phone away from the bed, and have something calming to do instead of scrolling. Our guide on [can't sleep after a breakup](/blog/articles/cant-sleep-after-a-breakup/) has more.
