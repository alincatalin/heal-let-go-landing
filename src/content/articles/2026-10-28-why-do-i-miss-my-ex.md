Missing someone and wanting them back are different things, even when they feel identical at 1am.

This post is only about the why. For a week-by-week plan, see [how to get over him](/blog/articles/how-to-get-over-him/). For the bigger picture, see [the 7 stages of a breakup](/blog/articles/7-stages-of-breakup/). Here we'll take the missing apart, because it's easier to sit with once you know what it's made of.

## What Are the 6 Reasons You Miss Your Ex?

Here they are at a glance. Each one gets its own section below.

| Reason | What's going on | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| Craving | Your brain's reward system still expects them | Cut the supply: mute them, stop checking |
| Attachment | They used to help calm you down | Let friends do some of the calming while you relearn it |
| Rosy memory | Memory keeps the good parts and drops the rest | A short list of real reasons it ended |
| Habit | Your days still point at them | Change one thing about the hardest time of day |

| Identity | Part of who you are got mixed up with them | Fill the gap with things that are yours |

## Is It Normal to Miss Your Ex This Much?

Yes. Even if you ended it. Even if they treated you badly. Even if it's been months and you thought you were past it.

People are often embarrassed by how much they miss an ex, as if it says something bad about their judgment. It doesn't. It says you were attached, and attachment takes a while to catch up with the news that it's over.

## Why Does Missing Them Feel Like Withdrawal?

Because, in a real sense, it is a kind of withdrawal.

When researchers scanned 15 people who had recently been dumped but were still in love, a photo of the ex activated brain regions tied to reward, craving and motivation, including areas also linked to cocaine addiction ([Fisher et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00784.2009)). Your brain learned that this person meant good things. Now the good things have stopped arriving, and it keeps asking for them.

That's why missing them can feel physical: the restless hands, the urge to check your phone, the ache in your chest when their name comes up. It's also why one look at their profile can undo a good afternoon. Each hit tells your brain the supply might still be there.

## What Does Attachment Have to Do With It?

A lot. Over months or years together, two people start to regulate each other. Your partner's voice calmed you down. Their presence at night helped you sleep. Your stress levels got tuned to theirs. It's one reason [a breakup hurts more at night](/blog/articles/why-breakups-hurt-more-at-night/).

Psychologists David Sbarra and Cindy Hazan describe this as coregulation: in a close relationship, partners' bodies and moods become linked, and losing that partner can leave your sleep and stress levels off balance for a while ([Sbarra and Hazan, 2008](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868308315702)). Recovery, in their account, mostly comes from learning to settle yourself again.

So when you miss them at the end of a bad day, part of what you miss is the person who used to bring your heart rate down. That's a real loss. Settling yourself is something you can relearn, and friends can do a lot of the calming in the meantime.

## Why Do I Only Remember the Good Times?

You remember the trip to the coast, the inside joke, the way they made coffee. You don't remember, or barely remember, the canceled plans and the knot in your stomach waiting for a reply.

There's a name for this. Researchers who followed people through a European holiday, a Thanksgiving break and a three-week bike trip found that people remembered each one more positively than they'd rated it while it was happening. They called it the "rosy view" ([Mitchell et al., 1997](https://doi.org/10.1006/jesp.1997.1333)). While you're living something, distractions and small disappointments drag it down. Later, those fade and the good parts stay.

That study was about trips, not exes. But it's easy to see the same thing at work after a breakup, when you're grieving and your mind keeps serving you the best reel.

What helps: write down three real reasons it ended, in plain words, while you still remember them clearly. Keep the list on your phone. When the highlight reel starts, read the list.

## Why Do I Miss Them Most at Certain Times?

Because habits outlast relationships.

If they texted you every morning, your thumb still reaches for the phone at 8am. If Sunday was your day together, Sunday will be the hardest. The song in the supermarket, the takeout you always ordered, the side of the bed you still don't sleep on: your days are full of cues that used to lead to them.

When one of these catches you, you haven't gone backwards. Your brain is running an old route, and old routes fade as you lay new ones over them: a different Sunday plan, a new morning playlist, someone else to text at 8am.

## Do I Miss Them, or Do I Just Feel Lonely?

A lot of what we call missing an ex is missing the shape of a relationship. Having a plus-one. Someone to tell about your day. Knowing who you'll spend the weekend with. Being chosen. Those needs are real, and they don't go away when the person does.

Try this the next time it hits. Finish the sentence "Right now I miss..." with something specific. If the answer is "someone to talk to," "a hug," or "plans for Saturday," some of that can come from other people, today. If the answer is something only they gave you, that's grief, and grief needs room more than it needs fixing.

## Why Do I Feel Like I Lost Part of Myself?

Because you probably did lose part of how you saw yourself, for now.

Couples merge. You pick up their music, their friends, their way of saying things. Your plans, your weekends and your idea of the future all had them in it. In three studies, Erica Slotter and colleagues found that breakups make people less clear about who they are, and that this confusion adds to the distress on top of missing the partner ([Slotter, Gardner and Finkel, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167209352250)).

So "I miss him" can partly mean "I miss who I was with him," or "I don't know who I am on a Tuesday night without him." That gap closes as you fill it with things that are yours. Old friends you drifted from. A hobby they never liked. A playlist with none of their songs on it.

## Why Do I Miss My Ex Even Though They Hurt Me?

Everything above still applies here. The craving doesn't check whether the person was good for you. The rosy view still edits the memories. Attachment still forms with someone who was sometimes kind and sometimes cruel, and the kind moments can feel extra precious because they weren't reliable. None of that means you were wrong to leave.

If the relationship swung between warmth and real harm, that pattern can make the attachment much harder to loosen, and it's worth talking to someone you trust about it. And if you're the kind of person who replays them for hours without much relationship to point to, [what is limerence?](/blog/articles/what-is-limerence/) may sound familiar.

## How Do I Stop Missing My Ex So Much?

You can't make it stop on command. You can make it smaller, and you can stop feeding it.

- Cut the supply. Every check of their profile is a small dose that keeps the craving topped up. Mute them, and read [how to stop checking their Instagram](/blog/articles/stop-checking-ex-instagram/) if it's a hard habit.
- Name it. Craving, habit, rosy memory, loneliness or identity? Knowing which one it is takes some of the force out of it.
- Give it a slot: twenty minutes a day to miss them on purpose: cry, write, replay. Outside that, "not now, later."
- Pick the time of day that hurts most and change one thing about it this week.
- Tell someone. A friend who knows you're struggling on Sundays can text you on Sundays.

## Does Missing Them Mean I Should Get Back Together?

Not on its own. Missing is a feeling made of all the things above, and every one of them shows up after breakups that were right as well as ones that weren't.

If, after real time apart, you still think the relationship could work, and you can name what would be different, that's worth thinking about in daylight. The quiz [should I text my ex?](/tools/should-i-text-my-ex/) is a good place to start. But how much you miss them isn't the test. Plenty of people miss an ex this much after a breakup that was the right call.

## FAQ: Why Do I Miss My Ex?

**Why do I miss my ex so much?**
Breakups activate the brain's reward and craving systems, so it keeps reaching for the person it learned to expect. On top of that you've lost someone who helped settle your mood, your habits still point at them, and memory keeps the good parts.

**Why do I miss my ex if I ended it?**
Ending a relationship doesn't end the attachment. You can know it was the right call and still miss the person, the routines and the future you'd pictured. Both are true at once, and that's normal.

**Why do I miss my ex even though they treated me badly?**
Attachment and craving don't check whether a person was good for you, and memory tends to keep the good moments. When kindness was unpredictable, those moments can feel even more precious. Missing them doesn't mean leaving was a mistake.

**Why can't I stop thinking about my ex?**
Your brain learned to expect them, so it keeps reaching for them, and every look at their profile tops that up. Habits and unanswered questions keep the loop going too. The thoughts usually get quieter with distance, not with willpower.

**Why do I miss my ex more as time goes on?**
Often because the first weeks were numbed by shock and busyness, and the loss only feels real once that wears off. Friends check in less, memory keeps softening the bad parts, and dates like birthdays come round. It usually peaks and then eases, especially if you're not checking on them.

**Is it normal to still miss my ex after 3 months?**
Yes. Missing an ex often comes in waves for months, set off by a song, a place or a bad day. What matters is whether it's easing overall, even slowly. If it isn't easing at all and you can't sleep or work, talk to a doctor or therapist.

**How long will I keep missing my ex?**
There's no fixed timeline. Many people notice it easing over the first month or two without contact, though bad days can show up for longer. Every check-in tends to top the craving back up, so distance helps.

**Does missing my ex mean I still love them?**
Sometimes, but not necessarily. Often you miss the comfort, the habits and the person you were in the relationship as much as the person themselves. Asking "what exactly do I miss right now?" can help you tell the difference.
