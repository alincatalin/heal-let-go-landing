You know it's over. Maybe they ended it. Maybe they don't love you the way you love them. Maybe they're not good for you and part of you knows it.

And you still love them. Your head has decided, and your heart hasn't caught up.

This guide is about closing that gap: why love lingers, what actually turns it down, and what to do with the love that's left.

## Can You Actually Stop Loving Someone?

**Not on command, but you can turn it down, and it fades.** Love isn't a switch. It's closer to a volume dial, and research shows you have some control over it.

Psychologists who study "love regulation" have found that people can deliberately make their feelings stronger or weaker by changing how they think about the person ([Langeslag and van Strien, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0161087)). You can't decide to feel nothing tomorrow. You can do things every day that make the feeling quieter, until one day you notice it's background noise.

What doesn't work: willpower alone, waiting for them to do something that makes you hate them, or staying in touch "until you're over it".

## Why Does Love Last After It's Over?

Three reasons, and each one has a fix.

**1. Love works like a craving.** In brain scans of people who had just been rejected but were still in love, seeing the ex activated reward and craving areas linked to addiction ([Fisher et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00784.2009)). Every text, photo or "just checking" is a small dose. That's why contact keeps love alive: in one study, love for an ex faded more slowly for people who stayed in contact ([Sbarra and Emery, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1350-4126.2005.00112.x)).

**2. Your memory is selective.** After a breakup you remember the best weekend, not the third cancelled plan. Love feeds on the highlight reel.

**3. They were part of who you are.** After a breakup, people's sense of who they are gets blurrier, and that confusion adds to the distress ([Slotter et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167209352250)). Letting go of them can feel like losing part of yourself.

If the love feels obsessive, like you can't function, read [what is limerence](/blog/articles/what-is-limerence/). If it's tied to a cycle of hurt and making up, read [what is a trauma bond](/blog/articles/what-is-a-trauma-bond/).

## What Actually Works: 5 Ways to Turn Love Down

| Strategy | What you do | What research says | The catch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Focus on what didn't work** | Write their real flaws and the moments that hurt. Read it when you idealize them | Thinking about an ex's negative sides lowered love feelings in a lab study ([Langeslag and Sanchez, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000360)) | Feels worse in the moment |
| **Distract yourself well** | Something absorbing: a friend, a game, a run, a show you love | Distraction improved mood, but didn't lower love ([Langeslag and Sanchez, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000360)) | Works for the hour, not the attachment |
| **Cut the supply** | No contact, mute and archive, photos into a hidden album | Contact and checking their profiles keep longing going ([Marshall, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1089/cyber.2012.0125)) | The first two weeks are hard |
| **Rebuild your own life** | Restart things that were yours, try something new | Recovering your sense of self came before feeling better ([Mason et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1475-6811.2011.01378.x)) | Slow, but it lasts |
| **Talk it out, privately** | Say everything to a friend, a journal or a coach. Not to them | Writing about a breakup reduced tension and physical symptoms ([Lepore and Greenberg, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1080/08870440290025768)) | Write to release, not to analyze forever |

**The trick is to use the first two together.** Reminding yourself of the bad parts loosens the attachment but makes you sad. Distraction lifts your mood but doesn't loosen anything. One after the other, you get some of both: ten minutes reading your list, then call a friend.

## A 10-Minute Exercise for When the Love Floods Back

When it hits, at night, after a song, when you see their name:

1. **Name it.** "I'm missing them. It's a wave. It'll pass."
2. **Read your list.** Three to five real things that didn't work. Not insults, moments: "They forgot my birthday dinner." "I always texted first." Our free [reasons not to text them](/tools/reasons-not-to-text/) tool makes this list your lock screen.
3. **Say what you want to say, somewhere safe.** Type the message you'd send them in our [unsent text tool](/tools/unsent-text/), or say it to Heal's [AI coach](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6754834593). Nothing reaches them.
4. **Switch to something absorbing** for ten minutes: a call, a walk, a game.
5. **Notice the wave going down.** It always does. Every time you ride it out without contacting them, the next one is a little weaker.

## How to Stop Loving Someone Who Doesn't Love You

This is the hardest version, because there's no ending to grieve, just a hope that won't die. A few things help:

- **Take their actions as the answer.** If they wanted to be with you, they would be. Not replying, keeping you at arm's length, or "I just don't feel the same" is the answer, even if it's not the one you want.
- **Stop auditioning.** Being extra available, extra understanding, extra patient doesn't make someone love you. It keeps you stuck waiting.
- **Grieve the hope, not just the person.** What you're losing is the future you imagined. That's a real loss.
- **Give it distance, even without a breakup.** No contact works for unrequited love too. Our guide on [how to get over someone you never dated](/blog/articles/how-to-get-over-someone-you-never-dated/) covers this case.

## How to Stop Loving Someone You Can't Be With

When there's a real reason you can't be together, like they're with someone else, you live in different countries, or you want different lives, the love isn't wrong. It just has nowhere to go.

Accept the love without acting on it. "I love them, and it's not going to happen" can both be true. The feeling fades faster when you stop arguing with it and stop feeding it. If you see them every day at work, keep it polite, brief and about work, and read [no contact with a coworker](/blog/articles/no-contact-with-coworker/).

## Will I Ever Stop Loving Them?

Most people do, or the love changes into something that doesn't hurt: a fondness, a memory, gratitude for what it taught you. In research that followed people after breakups, love for an ex faded over the following weeks and months, and faster for people who weren't in contact ([Sbarra and Emery, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1350-4126.2005.00112.x)).

You don't need to stop loving them completely to move on. You need it to be quiet enough that it doesn't run your days. For what that timeline looks like, see [how long it takes to get over a breakup](/blog/articles/how-long-does-it-take-to-get-over-a-breakup/), and for the full plan, [10 steps to get over a breakup](/blog/articles/how-to-get-over-a-breakup/).

## FAQ: How to Stop Loving Someone

**How do you unlove someone?**
You don't delete the feeling. You stop feeding it (no contact, no scrolling, no rereading old chats) and actively weaken it by remembering what didn't work. Over weeks and months, it loses its grip.

**How long does it take to stop loving someone?**
There's no set time. The sharpest longing often eases within the first one to three months without contact. Some fondness may stay much longer, and that's okay.

**Is it possible to love someone and still let them go?**
Yes, and often it's the kindest thing for both of you. Love is a feeling. Staying is a choice. You can feel one and choose differently.

**Why do I still love someone who hurt me?**
Because love and pain can live in the same place, especially when the good moments were very good. If the relationship swung between hurt and making up, the bond can feel even stronger. See [what is a trauma bond](/blog/articles/what-is-a-trauma-bond/).

**Should I tell them I still love them?**
Usually not. If they ended it, telling them rarely changes their mind and often resets your healing. Write it in an [unsent letter](/blog/articles/unsent-letter-to-ex/) instead.

**How do I stop loving my ex if we have kids?**
Keep every conversation about the kids, in writing where you can, short and polite. Low contact works when no contact can't. Put your emotional energy into friends, a therapist or a coach, not into them.

**What if I don't want to stop loving them?**
That's common at first. Part of you may still hope. Ask yourself what you'd tell a friend in the same position. Our [should I text my ex quiz](/tools/should-i-text-my-ex/) can help you check the hope against reality.
