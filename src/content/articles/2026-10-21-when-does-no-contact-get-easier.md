If you're asking this, today is probably a hard one. You might be on day 4 and wondering how anyone does this, or on day 19 and confused about why it suddenly hurts again.

It does get easier. Just not on a schedule, and not in a straight line.

## Why Are the First Days the Hardest?

Because your brain still expects them. The morning text, the evening call, the habit of telling them about your day. Each of those now lands on silence, and your mind keeps reaching for the person who used to be there.

That reaching is strongest early on. It eases as the old routines stop getting answered.

## Why Do I Feel Worse Again After Feeling Better?

This is the part nobody warns you about. You have three good days, and then a song in a shop knocks you flat.

That's normal. In a study that tracked young adults' feelings every day for a month after a breakup, emotions swung up and down along the way. Overall, love for the ex and sadness both eased over the month ([Sbarra and Emery, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1350-4126.2005.00112.x)). A bad day in the middle of good ones isn't you going backwards. It's what the path looks like.

## What Makes No Contact Get Easier Faster?

Mostly, taking away what keeps the wound open.

- **Stop checking their profiles.** People who kept looking at an ex's Facebook page reported more distress and longing ([Marshall, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1089/cyber.2012.0125)). Mute or block for now. Our guide on [how to stop checking their Instagram](/blog/articles/stop-checking-ex-instagram/) can help.
- **Don't test yourself.** "I feel fine, so one message is okay" is how a good week turns into a bad one.
- **Plan your hardest hour.** For most people it's late at night. Have something to do and someone to message.
- **Give the feelings somewhere to go.** Write the text you want to send somewhere it can't send.

## FAQ: When No Contact Gets Easier

**When does no contact get easier?**
For many people it starts to ease within the first couple of weeks, once the craving that peaks around day 3 has passed. By weeks three and four most people have more good days than bad, though dips still happen.

**Why is no contact getting harder instead of easier?**
Often because week two swaps craving for loneliness and doubt, which can feel worse even though you're progressing. Checking their profiles or a recent slip can also make it harder for a while.

**Is it normal to feel worse during no contact?**
Yes. Feelings swing up and down after a breakup, and a bad day after a few good ones is common. If it's not easing at all after several weeks, or you can't function, talk to a doctor or therapist.

**Does no contact get easier if they reach out?**
Usually not. Contact from them tends to restart the hoping and waiting. It gets easier with distance, not with messages.
