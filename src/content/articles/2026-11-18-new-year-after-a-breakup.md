Last New Year's you made plans together. This year the calendar flips and the first thing on your mind is still them.

You've probably seen the posts already. "New year, new me." Gym photos. Lists. It can feel like everyone else gets a clean slate and you're dragging last year into this one.

You can use January 1 too. Just not the way the posts suggest.

## Why Does the New Year Feel Like a Fresh Start?

Because your brain treats it like one. Researchers call it the "fresh start effect": after a clear line in time, like a new week, a new month, a birthday or a new year, people are more likely to go after the goals they care about. Searches for "diet" go up, gym visits go up, and people make more commitments to their goals ([Dai, Milkman and Riis, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901)).

Part of the reason is that a landmark lets you put the old you on the other side of it. Last year's version made the mistakes. This year's version gets to start over.

After a breakup, that's useful. It gives you a natural moment to stop living in the relationship's timeline and start your own. The trap is expecting the date to do the work.

## Do New Year's Resolutions Actually Work?

More often than their reputation says, if you make them well.

In one study, researchers followed 159 people who made New Year's resolutions and 123 people who wanted to change the same kind of thing but didn't make a resolution. At six months, 46% of the resolvers were still succeeding, compared with 4% of the non-resolvers. The people who believed they could do it, and who felt ready before January 1, did best ([Norcross, Mrykalo and Blagys, 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11920693/)).

A larger study of more than 1,000 people found that how you phrase the goal matters. Goals framed as something to move toward ("start running twice a week") were kept more often than goals framed as something to avoid ("stop eating sugar"): 58.9% against 47.1% ([Oscarsson and colleagues, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0234097)).

That second finding matters a lot after a breakup, because almost every breakup resolution is an avoidance goal. Don't text him. Don't check her stories. Don't cry about it.

## What Should My New Year Goals Be After a Breakup?

Keep the "don't" if it helps, but give each one a "do" that takes its place.

| Avoidance goal | Approach version | Why it's easier to keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Don't text my ex | Text a friend, or write it where it can't send, when I want to text them | You have something to do with the urge |
| Stop checking their Instagram | Open a different app I chose, like a book or a game, when I reach for it | The habit gets a new ending |
| Stop thinking about them | Write for five minutes each morning, then close the notebook | Thoughts get a set place instead of the whole day |
| Get over them by February | Reach day 30, then 60, of no contact | Days are something you can count, feelings aren't |
| Stop being sad | Plan one thing a week I look forward to | Builds something instead of fighting something |

Pick one or two. Not five. The people who keep resolutions are usually the ones who chose something they were already ready to do.

One more thing to keep in mind: "new me" doesn't mean you have to become someone else. Breakups often leave people less sure who they are, and that confusion is linked to more distress ([Slotter, Gardner and Finkel, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167209352250)). The goal for January isn't a new personality. It's getting back the parts of you that went quiet in the relationship. The friend you stopped calling, the hobby you dropped, the version of your weekend that was yours.

## How Do I Make the Plan Stick Past January?

Four things make the difference between a resolution and a plan.

1. **Write an if-then for the hard moment.** "If I want to text him after 10pm, then I'll open my notes and write it there." Plans written this way, naming the moment and the response, are followed much more often than plain intentions ([Gollwitzer and Sheeran, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1)).
2. **Plan four weeks, not twelve months.** A year is too big to feel. Four weeks is short enough to start and long enough to change something.
3. **Tell one person.** Not a public post. One friend who'll ask "how's it going?" in week two.
4. **Expect it to take a while.** In a study of people building a new daily habit, it took 66 days on average for the habit to feel automatic, with a wide range from person to person, and missing a single day didn't undo the progress ([Lally and colleagues, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)). If you slip on January 9, you haven't failed the year.

## What If I Still Feel Awful on January 1?

That's normal, and it doesn't mean the plan isn't working.

The holidays pack a lot of reminders into a few weeks: couples everywhere, family asking questions, midnight on New Year's Eve. If you've just got through that, January 1 might be the most tired you've felt in months. If you're dreading the night itself, our [Christmas and New Year after a breakup](/holidays/) page has a free tool or guide for each of the hard days.

Don't use the first day of the year as a scorecard. Use it as day one of a count. Early January can even feel lighter for some people, simply because the holiday cues stop. ## FAQ: New Year After a Breakup

**Is it a good idea to make New Year's resolutions after a breakup?**
Yes, if they're small and specific. Research found people who made resolutions were far more likely to have changed something six months later than people who wanted to change but didn't commit. Pick one or two, and phrase them as things you'll do.

**What's a good New Year's resolution after a breakup?**
One you can count. "Reach 30 days of no contact," "see a friend every week" or "write every morning in January" all work better than "get over him," which you can't measure and can't control. If dating again is on your list, check [the signs you're ready](/blog/articles/dating-after-a-breakup/) first.

**How do I stop thinking about my ex in the new year?**
You probably won't stop entirely, at least not at first. Give the thoughts a set place, like five minutes of writing each morning, and fill the evenings with plans that have a start time.

**Should I text my ex Happy New Year?**
Usually not, especially if you're doing no contact. One message tends to restart a conversation. Write it somewhere it can't send, like the free [unsent text tool](/tools/unsent-text/?occasion=newyear), and decide in the morning.

**How long does it take to feel like a new person after a breakup?**
There's no set time, and "new person" isn't really the goal. In habit research, new routines took around two months on average to feel automatic, so give January and February a fair try.

**What if I break my resolution in the first week?**
Start again the next day. Missing one day didn't undo progress in habit research, and a slip in no contact doesn't erase the days you already made it. A plan you restart is still a plan.
