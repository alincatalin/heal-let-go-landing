Before the list, one gentle thing. A lot of articles about "signs no contact is working" mean signs that your ex is coming back. This isn't one of those. No contact is for you, so the signs below are about you. If part of you is still hoping it brings them back, that's human, and we'll come to it.

## Is No Contact Working? A Quick Check

| Signs it's working | Signs it isn't working yet |
|---|---|
| Urges come less often and pass faster | You still watch their stories and profile |
| You forget to check their profile | You're counting days until they crack |
| They're not your first thought every morning | You break it every week or two |
| You say "I" more than "we" | Weeks in, you still replay it for hours a day |
| Bad days are further apart | You can't sleep, eat or work, and it isn't lifting |

Each one is explained below, along with what to change if you're stuck.

## What Does It Mean for No Contact to Be "Working"?

No contact works when it does what it's for: it gives the attachment time to loosen so you can think clearly again. Our [no contact rule guide](/blog/articles/no-contact-rule-guide/) covers the full rule; this page is about how to tell whether it's doing its job.

Measured that way, it can be working even if your ex hasn't noticed, hasn't texted and hasn't changed at all. You're the one it's working on.

## What Are the Signs No Contact Is Working?

These rarely arrive all at once, and most of them sneak up on you. You notice them looking back.

### 1. The urges are shorter

On day 3 the urge to text might have lasted all evening. By week three it might last ten minutes, then pass. You still get them. You just get through them faster, often without needing a plan.

### 2. You forgot to check

A morning when you didn't look at their profile, and you only realized at lunch. That's a big one. Checking keeps you in the loop: in a study of 464 people, those who kept looking at an ex's Facebook page reported more distress, more longing for the ex and less personal growth ([Marshall, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1089/cyber.2012.0125)). When the checking drops off on its own, the loop is loosening.

### 3. They're not the first thought every morning

Early on, you wake up and the breakup lands on you before you've opened your eyes. At some point you wake up, think about coffee or work first, and they turn up later. Or not until the afternoon.

### 4. You say "I" more than "we"

When you talk about your plans, your weekend or the breakup itself, you catch yourself saying "I" where you used to say "we." Researchers following people after breakups found that as people got clearer about who they were on their own, they used fewer "we" words when describing the breakup, and felt less lonely and less troubled by unwanted thoughts about it ([Larson and Sbarra, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550614563085)).

### 5. You can see the relationship more clearly

The highlight reel starts to include the bad bits. You remember the canceled plans as well as the trip. You can think about why it ended without wanting to argue with yourself about it.

### 6. Their silence stings less

At first, every day without a message from them can feel like a fresh rejection. Later you notice you haven't checked whether they've texted. You might even feel relieved they haven't.

### 7. You're making plans that have nothing to do with them

A pottery class, a weekend away, a new Sunday routine. You're doing it because you want to, and you didn't stop to wonder whether they'd see it on your story.

### 8. The bad days are further apart

You still have bad days. A song, an anniversary, a photo. But there's more space between them, and they don't wipe out the week.

## Why Does It Still Hurt if No Contact Is Working?

Because recovery isn't smooth, and it was never going to be.

In a study that had young adults rate their feelings at random moments for 28 days, those who had just broken up showed bigger swings in their emotions than people still in relationships, especially right after the breakup. Love for the ex faded steadily over the month, and sadness eased over time too. People who stayed in contact with the ex saw their love and sadness fade more slowly ([Sbarra and Emery, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1350-4126.2005.00112.x)).

Two things to take from that. Bad days in the middle of good weeks are normal. And contact slows things down, which is why you're doing this.

## What Are the Signs No Contact Isn't Working?

"Not working" usually means "not working yet," and most of the time something specific is getting in the way. Watch for these:

- **You're still watching them.** No messages, but you see every story, check who they follow and ask friends what they're up to. That's contact with one side switched off.
- **You're counting days to see if they crack.** If each day is really a countdown to their text, no contact has become a waiting game, and every day they stay silent will feel like losing.
- **You keep breaking it and starting over.** A few slips are normal. A pattern of texting every week or two means the urge needs more support than willpower alone.
- You're still replaying the relationship for hours a day after several weeks, with no easing at all.
- You can't function. You're not sleeping, eating or getting through work, and it isn't lifting.

If that last one is you, you haven't failed at no contact. You need more help than a rule can give, and that's okay to ask for. Please talk to a doctor or therapist, or find a local helpline at [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com) if you're struggling to cope or feel unsafe.

## What If They Haven't Reached Out? Does That Mean It Isn't Working?

No. Whether they reach out tells you something about them. It doesn't measure your progress.

Some exes get in touch during no contact and some never do, and neither one is a good measure of whether it's working for you. If the question keeps you up at night, our honest take on [what he's thinking during no contact](/blog/articles/what-is-he-thinking-during-no-contact/) may help.

If you've realized you're doing no contact mainly to get them back, you're not doing anything wrong. That hope is very common. It might help to keep going anyway and see what you want once the craving has quieted. Lots of people find the answer has changed by then.

## What Should I Do if No Contact Isn't Working?

Start by changing one thing.

- If you're still watching them, mute or block for now. Our guide on [how to stop checking their Instagram](/blog/articles/stop-checking-ex-instagram/) helps.
- If you keep breaking it, find your pattern. Most slips happen at the same time of day. Plan for that hour.
- If you're waiting for them, write down what you'd want from them if they did come back. Then ask whether you'd want that from anyone.
- If the relationship involved control or abuse and the pull feels impossible, read [what a trauma bond is](/blog/articles/what-is-a-trauma-bond/). That kind of attachment can take longer to loosen, and it's worth getting support for.
- If you're about to send something, run it through the [should I text my ex? quiz](/tools/should-i-text-my-ex/) first.

## FAQ: Signs No Contact Is Working

**How do I know if no contact is working?**
Look at your own patterns rather than your ex's. The urges to reach out get shorter and less frequent, you think about them less often, you stop checking their profiles and you start making plans for yourself. Bad days still happen, but they're further apart.

**How long does it take for no contact to start working?**
In one study that tracked people's feelings for four weeks after a breakup, love for the ex faded steadily over the month, with plenty of ups and downs along the way. Many people notice small changes in that first month, like shorter urges or a morning that doesn't start with them. Judge it over weeks, not days.

**Is it a sign no contact is working if my ex reaches out?**
Not really. Their reaching out says something about them, not about your healing. No contact is working if you're feeling steadier, whether or not they get in touch. If they come back with big apologies and promises, read [what is hoovering](/blog/articles/what-is-hoovering/) before you answer.

**Why do I feel worse during no contact?**
The first weeks often feel worse before they feel better, because you've cut off the thing your brain was craving. Feeling worse early on is usually withdrawal, not a sign of failure. If it doesn't ease or you can't function, talk to a doctor or therapist.

**Is it normal to miss my ex during no contact?**
Yes. Missing them is part of how the attachment loosens, and it can come in waves even when no contact is working. What changes is how long the missing lasts and how often it sends you to your phone.

**Does breaking no contact mean I have to start over?**
You start counting from today, but you don't lose what you learned in the days before. One slip doesn't undo weeks of progress. What matters is not following it with a second message.
