You're exhausted, but your body won't switch off. You fall asleep at 2am, wake at 4, and lie there replaying conversations. Or you sleep twelve hours and still wake up tired. Either way, the nights have become the worst part of the breakup.

Here's why it happens, and what actually helps.

## Why Can't I Sleep After a Breakup?

**Because your body is in stress mode and your mind is trying to solve the loss.** A breakup triggers the stress response: your heart rate and alertness go up, and that's the opposite of what you need to fall asleep. At the same time, your mind replays what happened, especially in the quiet of night.

Sleep problems are one of the most common breakup symptoms. In a study of 192 university students after a breakup, those with the most distress reported more sleep disturbance, more intrusive thoughts and more anxiety ([Field et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20432597/)).

| Sleep problem | What's often behind it | What helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can't fall asleep | Racing thoughts, phone in bed | A wind-down hour, write thoughts down, phone out of reach |
| Waking at 3 or 4am | Stress hormones, loneliness, an empty bed | Don't check your phone. Breathe, then get up if you're still awake after about 20 minutes |
| Dreaming about your ex | Your brain processing the loss | Normal. Write the dream down, don't text them about it |
| Sleeping too much | Low mood, wanting to escape | Same wake-up time every day, daylight in the morning |
| Can't sleep in your bed | Reminders everywhere | Change the bedding, move furniture, sleep on the other side |

For why emotions feel so much bigger after dark, read [why breakups hurt more at night](/blog/articles/why-breakups-hurt-more-at-night/). This guide is about the sleep itself.

## What to Do When You Can't Sleep Tonight

1. **Don't pick up your phone.** Their profile at 3am is a guaranteed way to stay awake and feel worse.
2. **Slow your breathing.** In for a count of four or five, out for longer. Do it ten times.
3. **Get the loop out of your head.** Keep a notebook by the bed. Write what's going round your mind, or the message you want to send them. If you need to type it, use a chat that can't send, like our [unsent text tool](/tools/unsent-text/) or Heal's [SOS](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6754834593), and then close it.
4. **If you're still awake after about 20 minutes, get up.** Go to another room, dim light, something boring: fold laundry, read a dull book. Go back to bed when you feel sleepy. Lying awake in bed teaches your brain that bed is for worrying.
5. **Don't try to force sleep.** Rest is still rest. Telling yourself "it's fine if I don't sleep tonight" often helps you fall asleep.

## What to Do in the Day So You Sleep at Night

Most sleep fixes happen during the day. The NHS recommends these habits for insomnia ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insomnia/)):

- **Get up at the same time every day**, even after a bad night. Don't sleep in.
- **Only go to bed when you're sleepy.**
- **Exercise during the day**, but not in the last few hours before bed.
- **Avoid caffeine and alcohol** for at least six hours before bed. Alcohol knocks you out but makes the second half of the night worse, and makes 2am texts more likely.
- **Skip naps**, or keep them very short.
- **Relax for an hour before bed** and avoid screens right before sleep.
- **Keep your bedroom dark, quiet and comfortable.**

Breakup-specific additions:

- **Make the bedroom yours again.** New bedding, a different side of the bed, their things out of sight. Here's [what to do with your ex's stuff](/blog/articles/what-to-do-with-your-exs-stuff/).
- **Mute them before bed.** Late-night scrolling of their profile is one of the main sleep killers. See [how to stop checking your ex's Instagram](/blog/articles/stop-checking-ex-instagram/).
- **Give worries a daytime slot.** Fifteen minutes in the early evening to write down everything that's bothering you, so it's less likely to ambush you at midnight.
- **Get morning daylight.** A walk outside in the first hour after waking helps reset your body clock.

## How Long Does Breakup Insomnia Last?

For most people, sleep starts to improve within a few weeks as the shock fades and routines return. The NHS treats sleep problems lasting under three months as short-term insomnia ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insomnia/)).

It's worth taking seriously if it drags on. In a study of 138 recently separated adults, sleep problems in roughly the first ten weeks after separation weren't linked to later health effects, but sleep problems that continued beyond that were linked to rising blood pressure over the following months ([Krietsch et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/hea0000089)). Short-term bad sleep after a breakup is normal. Months of it is a reason to get help.

## Should I Take Something to Sleep?

Talk to a doctor or pharmacist before taking anything. According to the NHS, sleeping pills are now rarely prescribed for insomnia, and only for a short time. Over-the-counter sleep aids can't cure insomnia and shouldn't be used for more than a week or two ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insomnia/)). The treatment that works best long-term is a form of talking therapy (CBT) for sleep, which a doctor can refer you to or which is available as online programmes.

Using alcohol to sleep is especially common after a breakup and especially unhelpful.

## When to See a Doctor

- You've been sleeping badly for more than a few weeks and habit changes haven't helped
- Poor sleep is affecting your work, driving or safety
- You're sleeping far too much and struggling to get out of bed
- Sleep problems come with low mood, hopelessness or anxiety most days. See [depression after a breakup](/blog/articles/depression-after-a-breakup/) and [anxiety after a breakup](/blog/articles/anxiety-after-a-breakup/)

## FAQ: Can't Sleep After a Breakup

**Is it normal not to sleep after a breakup?**
Yes. Sleep problems are one of the most common reactions to a breakup, especially in the first few weeks. They usually improve as the shock fades.

**Why do I wake up at 3am after a breakup?**
Stress hormones, a quiet house and an empty bed make early-morning waking common. Your mind fills the silence with the breakup. Don't check your phone. Breathe, and get up if you're still awake after about 20 minutes.

**Why do I keep dreaming about my ex?**
Dreams often replay what's on your mind, and right now that's them. It doesn't mean you should get back together or contact them. It usually fades with time.

**Why am I sleeping so much after a breakup?**
Sleeping a lot can be a way to escape painful feelings, or a sign of low mood. Keep a fixed wake-up time and get daylight early. If it continues for weeks, talk to a doctor.

**How can I sleep alone after a breakup?**
Change something about the bed: new sheets, the middle of the bed, an extra pillow. Use a podcast or calm audio with a timer. It feels strange at first, and it gets easier.

**What should I do when I can't stop thinking about my ex at night?**
Write the thoughts down, then do something calm and boring. Our guide on [how to stop thinking about your ex](/blog/articles/how-to-stop-thinking-about-your-ex/) has a section on nights.
