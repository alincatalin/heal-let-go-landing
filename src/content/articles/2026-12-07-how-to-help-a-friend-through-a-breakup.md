Your friend just went through a breakup. Maybe they called you crying. Maybe they've gone quiet. You want to help, and you're not sure what to say without making it worse.

The good news: you don't need perfect words. You need to show up, listen, and keep showing up. Here's how.

## How Can You Help a Friend Through a Breakup?

**Be there, listen, and help with the practical stuff, for longer than you think.** Most people get lots of support in the first few days and much less by week three, when it often hurts the most.

Support from friends matters. In a large survey of over 5,000 people, those who coped best with a breakup were more likely to lean on friends and family as a safe base ([Davis, Shaver and Vernon, 2003](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167203252884)). And close relationships matter for health in general: a review of 148 studies found people with stronger social ties had about 50% higher odds of survival over the study periods ([Holt-Lunstad, Smith and Layton, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316)).

You can't fix their heartbreak. You can make sure they're not going through it alone.

## What Should You Say to a Friend After a Breakup?

Simple, honest words work best. Some that help:

- "I'm so sorry. That sounds really hard."
- "Do you want to talk about it, or do you want a distraction?"
- "You don't have to be okay right now."
- "I'm here. Text me any time, even late."
- "Want me to come over?"
- "It makes sense that you miss them."

## What Shouldn't You Say?

These are usually meant kindly, but they tend to land badly:

| Instead of | Why it can hurt | Try |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Plenty more fish in the sea" | Dismisses the loss of this person | "I'm sorry you're losing someone you loved" |
| "I never liked them anyway" | Can make them feel judged for their choices | "How are you feeling about it today?" |
| "You'll be over it in no time" | Puts a clock on their grief | "Take all the time you need" |
| "At least you didn't marry them" | Shrinks the pain | "That's a big loss" |
| "You should just get back out there" | Pushes them before they're ready | "What would feel good this week?" |
| "Have you checked their Instagram?" | Feeds the urge they're trying to resist | "Want me to help you mute them?" |

If they ask your honest opinion of their ex, it's fine to be honest, kindly. Just know they may get back together, and harsh words can be hard to take back.

## Practical Ways to Help

Vague offers like "let me know if you need anything" put the work on them. Specific offers are easier to say yes to:

1. **Bring food.** Breakups wreck appetites. A meal, snacks, a coffee on the way to work.
2. **Make a plan for the hard times.** Weekend evenings, the first holiday, their ex's birthday. "Saturday, you're with me."
3. **Help with the logistics.** Collecting their things, moving out, boxing up the ex's stuff. Our guide on [what to do with your ex's stuff](/blog/articles/what-to-do-with-your-exs-stuff/) has a plan.
4. **Be the person they text instead.** Tell them: "If you want to text your ex, text me first." Then answer.
5. **Get them outside.** A walk, a class, a game. Movement and daylight help mood, and it's a change of scene.
6. **Help with their phone, if they ask.** Muting their ex, archiving the chat, hiding photos. See [how to stop checking their Instagram](/blog/articles/stop-checking-ex-instagram/).
7. **Remember the dates.** Mark the one-month point, their ex's birthday, the holidays. Check in on those days without being asked.

## How Do You Help Them Stop Texting Their Ex?

This is often the hardest part, and the place where a friend can help most.

- **Don't lecture.** They know it's a bad idea. Shame makes them hide it, not stop.
- **Ask what they hope will happen.** Often the answer shows them the message won't give them that.
- **Suggest waiting.** "Write it, wait until tomorrow, and show me first." Most urges pass.
- **Share a tool.** Our [should I text my ex quiz](/tools/should-i-text-my-ex/) helps them decide calmly, and the [no contact rule guide](/blog/articles/no-contact-rule-guide/) explains why space helps.
- **If they slip, be kind.** One message isn't the end. Our post on [what to do after you break no contact](/blog/articles/i-broke-no-contact/) is a good one to send them.

## How Long Should You Keep Checking In?

**Longer than feels necessary.** The first week gets lots of attention. Weeks three to six often feel lonelier, because everyone else has moved on while the pain is still there.

A simple rhythm:

- **First week:** daily, even just a text.
- **Weeks two to six:** a few times a week, plus one plan together.
- **After that:** check in around hard dates, and keep inviting them to things.

If they want a sense of what's typical, [how long it takes to get over a breakup](/blog/articles/how-long-does-it-take-to-get-over-a-breakup/) is a reassuring read. For things they can do themselves, share our [self-care after a breakup checklist](/blog/articles/self-care-after-a-breakup/).

## When Should You Be Worried?

Sadness, crying, poor sleep and not eating much are common in the first weeks. Be more concerned if your friend:

- Talks about not wanting to be here, or about hurting themselves
- Can't get out of bed, eat or work for weeks
- Is drinking or using drugs much more than usual
- Withdraws from everyone, including you
- Is being harassed, threatened or controlled by the ex

If they talk about suicide or self-harm, take it seriously. Stay with them, and help them get support right away. In the US, they can call or text 988. Elsewhere, find a free helpline at [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com). If they're in immediate danger, call emergency services.

For signs that sadness has become something more, see our guide to [depression after a breakup](/blog/articles/depression-after-a-breakup/).

## Look After Yourself Too

Supporting someone through heartbreak can be draining, especially if it goes on for months. It's okay to set limits: "I can't talk tonight, but I'll call you tomorrow." Share the load with other friends. You'll be more helpful if you're not running on empty.

## FAQ: Helping a Friend Through a Breakup

**What do you text a friend after a breakup?**
Keep it simple and warm: "I just heard. I'm so sorry. I'm here if you want to talk, or if you want a distraction. No need to reply." Then follow up in a day or two.

**Should I tell my friend I never liked their ex?**
Usually not straight away. They're grieving, and they may get back together. If they ask directly, be honest but gentle, and focus on how they were treated rather than on the ex as a person.

**How do I cheer up a friend after a breakup?**
Don't push for cheerful. Offer company and small, low-effort plans: a film, a walk, food. Laughter tends to come back on its own when they feel supported, not when they're told to cheer up.

**What if my friend keeps going back to their ex?**
Stay close and avoid "I told you so." Ask what they want and what they're hoping will be different. If the relationship is harmful, keep the door open so they know they can come to you.

**What if I'm friends with both of them?**
Don't pass messages or news between them. Spend time with each separately, and don't take sides publicly. Tell your friend you won't talk about them with their ex, and keep to it.
