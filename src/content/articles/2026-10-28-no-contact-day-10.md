Ten days. Double digits. If you've made it here, you've already done the part most people find hardest: the first week, the [day 3 peak](/blog/articles/hardest-day-no-contact/) and your first weekend without them.

Day 10 usually feels calmer than the first week. It also brings a quieter kind of risk.

## What Does Day 10 of No Contact Feel Like?

Common day 10 experiences:

- **Shorter urges.** They still come, but more often they pass in minutes instead of taking the whole evening.
- **Real gaps.** A morning or an afternoon where they weren't on your mind. Then, sometimes, guilt about that.
- **Softer memories.** The good times are easier to remember than the reasons it ended.
- **Loneliness.** With less energy going into resisting, you notice the empty hours you used to spend together.
- **Doubt.** "Maybe I overreacted." "Maybe it wasn't that bad."

None of this is a schedule. Some people have their worst day on day 9 or 12. If yours looks different, you're not behind.

## Why Does "Maybe I Overreacted" Show Up Now?

Because distance makes the highlight reel easier to watch than the full film. Ten days out, you're no longer living with the things that went wrong, so it's the good parts that come to mind.

It also helps to know that ups and downs are expected. In a study that had young adults rate their feelings at random moments for 28 days after a breakup, their emotions swung more than those of people still in relationships, while love for the ex and sadness both eased over the month. People who stayed in contact with the ex saw their love and sadness fade more slowly ([Sbarra and Emery, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1350-4126.2005.00112.x)).

So a soft, nostalgic evening on day 10 isn't a sign you made a mistake. It's a normal swing, and contact would slow the overall easing down.

**What helps:** reread the reasons list you wrote on [day 1](/blog/articles/no-contact-day-1/). Add anything you'd started to forget. If you never wrote one, write three real things that didn't work now, while you still remember them.

## How Do I Know It's Working by Day 10?

Look at your own patterns, not at whether they've reached out:

- You let at least one urge pass without acting on it.
- You had a stretch of hours where you felt okay.
- You checked their profile less than in the first week, or not at all. That matters: in a study of 464 people, those who kept looking at an ex's Facebook page reported more distress and more longing for the ex ([Marshall, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1089/cyber.2012.0125)).
- You remembered something real about the relationship, not just the good parts.
- You caught yourself saying "I" where you used to say "we." Researchers found that as people got clearer about who they were on their own after a breakup, they used fewer "we" words and felt less lonely ([Larson and Sbarra, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550614563085)).

That's what progress looks like this early. More signs are in [signs no contact is working](/blog/articles/signs-no-contact-is-working/).

## What Is He Thinking on Day 10 of No Contact?

Honestly, nobody can tell you. Ten days in, he might feel relief, regret, both, or be too busy to have noticed much. Some exes get in touch around now and many don't, and neither tells you how you're doing.

When the question loops, turn it around: what are you hoping the answer is, and what would you do with it? If the answer is "reach out," that's the urge talking. More in [what he's thinking during no contact](/blog/articles/what-is-he-thinking-during-no-contact/).

## FAQ: No Contact Day 10

**Does no contact get easier after 10 days?**
For many people, yes. Urges are usually shorter and less frequent by now than in the first week. It still goes up and down, so a bad evening on day 10 or 12 is normal and doesn't undo your progress.

**Why do I miss my ex more on day 10?**
Often because the panic of the first week has faded, which leaves more room for sadness and nostalgia. Memory also softens the hard parts first. Missing them is part of how the attachment loosens. More in [why you miss your ex so much](/blog/articles/why-do-i-miss-my-ex/).

**What is he thinking after 10 days of no contact?**
Nobody can know. He might feel relief, regret or both. Whether he reaches out says something about him, not about whether no contact is working for you.

**Should I reach out after 10 days of no contact?**
If the main reason is that you miss them, wait. That feeling usually eases over the coming weeks. Our [no contact rule guide](/blog/articles/no-contact-rule-guide/) treats 30 days as the minimum. If you think there's a practical reason to get in touch, take the [should I text my ex quiz](/tools/should-i-text-my-ex/) first.
