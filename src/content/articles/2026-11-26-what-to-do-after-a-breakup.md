When a relationship ends, the hardest question is often the smallest one: what do I do now? Tonight, tomorrow morning, this weekend.

This is a practical checklist, in the order you'll need it. You don't have to do all of it. Pick what fits, tick it off, and come back when you're ready for the next part.

## What Should You Do Right After a Breakup?

**Three things, today:** tell two people you trust, mute your ex on every app, and get through tonight without contacting them. Everything else can wait.

Here's the whole checklist at a glance:

| When | Do this | Why it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 | Tell two friends. Mute your ex everywhere. Eat something | You're not alone, and the triggers stop arriving |
| Days 2 to 3 | Archive the chat. Move photos to a hidden album. Plan one thing for each evening | The craving often peaks around day 3 |
| Week 1 | Sort shared logistics in one message. Set a bedtime. Write your feelings down | Fewer reasons to talk, better sleep, less in your head |
| Weeks 2 to 4 | Pick up one old hobby. See a friend twice a week. Make a "why it ended" list | Rebuilds the parts of you that were yours |
| Month 2 to 3 | Try something new. Change one thing in your space. Notice what you don't miss | You start building a life that isn't about them |

## Day 1: The First 24 Hours

The first day is about protection, not healing.

- [ ] **Tell two people.** A text is enough: "We broke up. Can you check on me tonight?"
- [ ] **Mute or unfollow them on every app.** Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp status, Spotify. You don't have to block yet.
- [ ] **Don't send the "one last message".** If you need to say it, write it in our free [unsent text tool](/tools/unsent-text/). It looks like a chat and never sends.
- [ ] **Eat something and drink water.** Even if it's toast.
- [ ] **Plan tonight.** A friend over, a film, a long shower, a call. Nights are hardest.
- [ ] **Don't make big decisions.** No moving out, quitting or big announcements today.

Our [no contact day 1 guide](/blog/articles/no-contact-day-1/) covers this first day in more detail.

## The First Week

The shock starts wearing off, and the pull to reach out gets stronger. Many people find day three the hardest. Here's [why the third day is so hard](/blog/articles/hardest-day-no-contact/).

- [ ] **Archive the chat and hide the photos.** Out of sight, not deleted.
- [ ] **Sort logistics in one go.** Keys, belongings, bills, a shared subscription. One clear message, then done. Our guide to [what to do with your ex's stuff](/blog/articles/what-to-do-with-your-exs-stuff/) has a plan.
- [ ] **Write it out.** Every night for ten minutes, write what you feel. In a randomized study, students who wrote about their breakup avoided the rise in tension, fatigue and physical symptoms that others had ([Lepore and Greenberg, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1080/08870440290025768)).
- [ ] **Set a bedtime and keep your phone out of reach.** See [why breakups hurt more at night](/blog/articles/why-breakups-hurt-more-at-night/).
- [ ] **Move every day.** A walk counts.
- [ ] **Decide on contact.** If you can, go no contact. If you share kids or work, keep it to logistics. The [no contact rule guide](/blog/articles/no-contact-rule-guide/) explains how.
- [ ] **Start counting.** Day 1, day 2, day 3. Seeing the number grow is proof you're getting through it. Heal's [no contact tracker](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6754834593) does this for you and keeps an SOS button ready for the moments you want to reach out.

## The First Month

Now the work shifts from surviving to rebuilding.

- [ ] **See people twice a week.** Strong social ties are one of the best-documented protectors of health. A review of 148 studies found people with stronger social relationships had 50% higher odds of survival over the study periods ([Holt-Lunstad et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316)). After a breakup, friends are medicine.
- [ ] **Make a "why it ended" list.** Five real moments that didn't work. Read it when you start romanticizing. Our [reasons not to text them](/tools/reasons-not-to-text/) tool turns it into your lock screen.
- [ ] **Stop checking their profiles.** People who kept looking at an ex's Facebook page reported more distress and longing ([Marshall, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1089/cyber.2012.0125)). Here's [how to stop checking their Instagram](/blog/articles/stop-checking-ex-instagram/).
- [ ] **Pick up one thing you dropped.** A sport, an instrument, a friend you saw less.
- [ ] **Plan the hard dates.** Their birthday, your anniversary, holidays. Decide in advance what you'll do that day.
- [ ] **Take on a challenge.** Our free [30-day no contact challenge](/tools/no-contact-challenge/) gives you one five-minute task a day.

## Months Two and Three

- [ ] **Try something new.** A class, a trip, a different route to work. New experiences help you build a sense of self that isn't tied to them. After a breakup, a blurry sense of who you are adds to the distress ([Slotter et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167209352250)).
- [ ] **Change your space.** Move the furniture, new bedding, a plant. Small changes break the link between the room and them.
- [ ] **Reflect, once you're steadier.** In one study, young adults who repeatedly reflected on their breakup over nine weeks rebuilt a clearer sense of self and felt less lonely ([Larson and Sbarra, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550614563085)). Ask what you learned and what you want next time.
- [ ] **Notice what you don't miss.** The arguments, the waiting, the walking on eggshells.
- [ ] **Decide about dating** when you want someone new, not just someone to fill the space.

## 30 Things to Do After a Breakup

When you have an empty evening and don't know what to do with it, pick one.

**To get through tonight**
1. Call the friend who makes you laugh
2. Take a long shower or bath
3. Rewatch a comfort show you loved before you met them
4. Go for a walk with a podcast
5. Write the text you want to send, and don't send it
6. Cook something simple and eat it at a table
7. Put on music that isn't "your song"
8. Go to bed early, phone in another room

**To feel like yourself again**
9. Restart a hobby you dropped
10. Make a playlist that's only yours
11. See friends you saw less while you were together
12. Rearrange your room
13. Try a new haircut or a small style change
14. Go somewhere you've never been with them
15. Read a book you've been meaning to read
16. Start a 30-day challenge

**To move your body**
17. A run or walk at the same time each day
18. A class you can drop in on: yoga, climbing, boxing
19. A weekend hike
20. Dance in your kitchen

**To look forward**
21. Plan a trip, even a small one
22. Sign up for a class
23. Volunteer somewhere once
24. Set one goal for the next 90 days
25. Write a list of what you want in your next relationship

**To clear things out**
26. Box up their things
27. Unsubscribe from shared accounts
28. Change passwords they knew
29. Delete or archive the photos you keep scrolling to
30. Return or donate gifts you don't want to keep. See [what to do with gifts from your ex](/blog/articles/what-to-do-with-gifts-from-ex/)

## What Not to Do After a Breakup

The short list: don't text them to "talk", don't try to stay friends straight away, don't keep checking their profiles, don't drink to sleep, and don't jump into something new just to forget them. Our [10 steps to get over a breakup](/blog/articles/how-to-get-over-a-breakup/) explains why each of these backfires, with a full table.

If you're not getting through the day after a few weeks (not sleeping, not eating, not working, or feeling hopeless), talk to a doctor or therapist. If you have any thoughts of harming yourself, contact a crisis line now: [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com) lists free lines for your country, and in the US you can call or text 988.

## FAQ: What to Do After a Breakup

**What's the first thing to do after a breakup?**
Tell someone you trust, and mute your ex. Those two steps protect you from the two things that hurt most in the first days: being alone with it, and seeing them everywhere.

**Should I delete my ex's number?**
You don't have to. Many people archive the chat and rename the contact ("Don't"). If you keep reaching out, deleting or blocking can help. See [should I block my ex](/blog/articles/should-i-block-my-ex/).

**What should I not do after a breakup?**
Don't text them, don't stalk their profiles, don't make big decisions in the first week, and don't numb it with alcohol. Each of these makes the pain last longer.

**How do I keep myself busy after a breakup?**
Plan your evenings and weekends in advance, since those are the hardest times. The list of 30 things above is a good place to start. The goal isn't to never feel it. It's to have something to do after you have.

**Is it okay to cry every day after a breakup?**
In the first weeks, yes. If you're still crying all day after several weeks and nothing feels lighter, talk to a doctor or therapist.

**How long does it take to feel better?**
Usually less time than it feels. Many people feel a real shift within one to three months. Our [breakup recovery timeline](/blog/articles/how-long-does-it-take-to-get-over-a-breakup/) goes week by week.
