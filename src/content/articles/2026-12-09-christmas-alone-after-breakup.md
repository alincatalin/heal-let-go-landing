Last year there were two stockings. This year the apartment is quiet, the group chat is full of family photos, and the only plan you have for the 25th is "get through it."

That's a plan, but it's not much of one. Here's how to make the day feel like yours, hour by hour.

## Is Being Alone on Christmas the Same as Being Lonely?

No, though after a breakup they tend to show up together.

Being alone is a situation. Being lonely is a feeling. You can feel lonely at a table of twelve, and you can spend a quiet day by yourself and feel mostly okay.

Research on solitude backs up that difference. In a set of experiments, people who spent 15 minutes sitting alone came out calmer and less wound up, both less excited and less anxious. It wasn't all good: some felt a little lonelier and sadder too, and that dip was smaller when they chose what to think about ([Nguyen, Ryan and Deci, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167217733073)). And people got more of the relaxing side when they had chosen to be alone.

So the goal isn't to avoid being alone. It's to make it a day you chose and shaped, not one that happens to you.

## How Do I Plan Christmas Day When I'm Alone?

Plan it in blocks. An empty day has no edges, and the spiral usually starts in the gaps. A planned day has handholds.

Here's a sample. Swap anything that doesn't fit you.

| Time | Block | Why it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Morning | A walk outside, even 20 minutes | Daylight and movement before your phone gets you |
| Late morning | A slow breakfast you'd never make on a weekday | Something to look forward to that's just yours |
| Lunch | A call or video call with a friend or relative | One booked conversation means the day isn't silent |
| Early afternoon | A project: a puzzle, baking, a long bath, a game | Hands busy, head quieter |
| Late afternoon | Get out again, or meet someone also on their own | This is the hardest stretch (see below) |
| Evening | A proper dinner and a film you've been saving | A clear end to the day |
| Night | Phone on charge in another room | Fewer 1am decisions |

Book the lunch call now, not on the morning. "Can I call you around 1 on Christmas?" is an easy text to send in early December and a hard one to send on the day.

## Why Are the Quiet Hours the Hardest?

For many people the worst stretch is the late afternoon. The presents are done, the light goes, everyone else seems to be sitting down to dinner, and the day has nothing left in it.

Then night arrives, and nights after a breakup have their own weight. We wrote about [why breakups hurt more at night](/blog/articles/why-breakups-hurt-more-at-night/) if that's when it hits you.

Plan the quiet hours like you'd plan a hard meeting:

- **Put something with a start time there.** A movie at 4, a walk at 4:30, a call with a friend in another time zone.
- **Change rooms or leave the house.** Same couch, same light, same thoughts.
- **Cook something that takes effort.** Chopping, stirring and timing keep your hands and attention busy.
- **Have one "break glass" person.** Someone who's said "call me if it gets bad, even on Christmas." Ask them now.

## Should I Volunteer on Christmas?

It's worth a look. Volunteering gives the day structure, puts you around people, and makes the hours about someone else for a while.

A few things to know:

- **Ask early.** Christmas Day shifts can fill up fast. The days around Christmas may be easier to get, and the need doesn't stop on the 26th.
- **Don't do it as a performance.** You don't need to post about it, and it doesn't have to fix anything. One shift is enough.
- **Pick a shift that ends in the afternoon.** That way it covers part of the quiet hours instead of leaving them all to you.

If volunteering isn't possible, a smaller version still helps: drop a card at a neighbor's, check on an older relative, call someone you know is also on their own.

## Who Else Is on Their Own on Christmas?

More people than you'd think from your feed. A friend who works abroad. A coworker whose family is far away. Someone else who went through a breakup this year.

You don't have to make it a big event. Try:

> "I'm doing Christmas low-key this year. If you're around too, want to get food or watch something?"

Two people who'd each be alone can make a perfectly good Christmas together. Lots of good traditions started that way.

## What About Social Media on Christmas?

Christmas is the day your feed is at its most coupled. Matching pajamas, engagement posts, family photos, and maybe one from your ex.

Looking hurts more than it seems. In one study of 464 people, those who kept checking their ex's Facebook page reported more distress over the breakup, more longing for their ex and less personal growth ([Marshall, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1089/cyber.2012.0125)).

Before the day:

- **Mute your ex** on every app, plus anyone who often posts them: their siblings, their best friend, the mutual friend who always has a camera out.
- **Move social apps off your home screen** for the 24th to the 26th.
- **Post nothing for them to see.** If you catch yourself picking a photo because they might notice it, put the phone down.

## What Do I Do When the Urge to Text Them Hits?

It probably will. Christmas makes reaching out feel allowed. It's one line, it's polite, it's the season.

But be honest about what it's for. Usually "Merry Christmas" means "are you thinking about me too?" And then the whole evening becomes about whether they reply.

When the urge hits:

1. **Name it.** "I want to text them because I'm lonely and it's 5pm on Christmas." Saying what's happening takes some of the heat out.
2. **Wait 20 minutes.** Set a timer. Do something with your hands.
3. **Write it where it can't send.** Our free [unsent text tool](/tools/unsent-text/?occasion=christmas) has a Christmas mode for exactly this. Get every word out, then close it.
4. **Call your "break glass" person** if it's still loud.

## A Note If Christmas Feels Like Too Much

Some people find the holidays alone bring up more than sadness. If you're struggling to cope, or you're having thoughts of harming yourself, please reach out today, even on Christmas. In the US, you can call or text 988 to reach the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline. Elsewhere, find a local helpline at [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com). If you're in immediate danger, call your local emergency number.

## FAQ: Christmas Alone After a Breakup

**How do I spend Christmas alone after a breakup?**
Plan the day in blocks: a morning walk, a booked call at lunch, a project in the afternoon, a proper dinner and a film. Mute your ex beforehand and keep your phone in another room at night.

**Is it normal to feel worse at Christmas after a breakup?**
Yes. The day is full of traditions and reminders, and couples and families are everywhere. Feeling worse in late December doesn't mean you're going backwards.

**Is it okay to want to be alone for the holidays?**
Yes. Choosing a quiet Christmas can be restful. It helps to plan it, keep one call in the day and have someone to reach if it gets heavy.

**Should I text my ex on Christmas if I'm alone?**
Usually not. Being alone makes the urge stronger, but a "Merry Christmas" text tends to restart contact. Write it somewhere it can't send, wait, and call a friend instead.

**What can I do on Christmas Day alone?**
Walk, cook something slow, volunteer a shift, start a puzzle, watch a film you've saved, or meet up with someone else who's on their own. Anything with a start time helps.
