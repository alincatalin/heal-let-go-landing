The invite says 7pm, ugly sweaters optional. You already know who else is on the list. You've opened the RSVP four times and closed it four times, because the question isn't really "am I free on the 14th?" It's "can I stand in a room with them?"

Here's how to answer that, and how to get through the night if the answer is yes.

## Should I Go to a Party If My Ex Will Be There?

You don't have to. Skipping one party is not losing, and nobody who matters is keeping score.

Ask yourself three things before you decide.

- **How fresh is it?** In the first few weeks, seeing them can undo a lot of hard work. If it's been months and you mostly feel tired of the whole thing, going may be fine.
- **Why do you want to go?** If the honest answer is "to see them," or "so they see me looking great," that's a reason to stay home. If it's "these are my friends and I want to see them," go.
- **Can you leave?** If you can drive yourself or get a ride home whenever you want, the night is much easier to handle.

This is harder than it sounds. When researchers scanned the brains of people who had recently been rejected and were still in love, looking at a photo of the person who left them activated areas linked to reward, craving and addiction ([Fisher et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00784.2009)). A photo did that. A real person in a party sweater, laughing at the snack table, is a lot stronger.

## What If My Ex Is a Coworker?

The office party is the hardest one to skip, and often you shouldn't have to. It's your job too.

If you work together, you're probably already doing work-only contact, and the party is just a louder, boozier version of the office. Keep it the same: polite, brief, no talk about the relationship. Our guide to [no contact with a coworker](/blog/articles/no-contact-with-coworker/) covers the daily version.

A few extra things for the work party:

- **Go for the part that counts.** Show up for the speech, the toast or the first hour. You can leave before the dancing.
- **Stick with your own team.** Pick one or two coworkers you like and stay near them.
- **Remember Monday.** Whatever happens tonight, you'll be in a meeting with these people next week. That's a good reason to stay sober-ish and say less.

## What If We Share the Same Friends?

This is the party where the rules feel fuzzy. Your friends invited both of you, maybe on purpose, maybe because they didn't want to pick.

Ask the host, kindly: "Is Sam coming? No pressure either way, I just want to know so I can plan." Most friends would rather tell you than watch you find out at the door.

If you go, bring a friend who knows the whole story. Their job is simple: stay near you, notice when you're spiraling, and walk you to the kitchen if your ex heads your way.

## What's a Good Plan for the Night?

Make the decisions before you get there, when you can think clearly. Once you're in the room, you'll just follow the plan.

1. **Pick your arrival.** Come a little after the start, so you walk into a full room, not an empty one.
2. **Pick your person.** One friend who knows, and a signal for "get me out of this conversation."
3. **Pick your drink limit.** Write the number in your phone notes. More on this below.
4. **Pick your leaving time.** Tell your friend. Set an alarm if it helps.
5. **Pick your line.** One sentence for if they come over (see the next section).
6. **Mute them before you leave the house.** Their stories and posts tonight will be full of the same party you're at. You don't need to watch it twice.

## What Do I Say If My Ex Comes Over?

Keep it short, polite and finished. You're closing the conversation, not opening one.

> "Hey. Happy holidays. I'm going to go find Jess."

> "Hi. Hope you're doing well." Then turn back to the person you were talking to.

> "Good to see you. Enjoy the party."

You don't need to explain yourself, catch up or prove you're fine. If they try to start something deeper ("Can we talk for a minute?"), you can say:

> "Not tonight. Have a good one."

Then go. To the bathroom, the kitchen, the porch, your friend. A hello at a party doesn't break no contact. A long talk in the coat room does. If you're unsure where the line sits, see [what counts as breaking no contact](/blog/articles/what-counts-as-breaking-no-contact/).

## What If They Bring Someone New?

This is the scenario people dread most, so plan for it even if you think it won't happen.

If you know ahead of time, it's a fair reason to skip. You don't have to watch that in person to prove anything.

If it happens without warning:

- **Don't study the new person.** Their outfit, their laugh, whether they're "better" than you. That's a comparison you can't win and don't need to make.
- **Give yourself ten minutes.** Step outside, run cold water over your wrists, breathe out slowly a few times. Then decide whether to stay.
- **Leave if you need to.** "I'm not feeling great, I'm going to head out" is a full explanation.

Whatever their new relationship means, it isn't a verdict on you.

## Should I Drink at a Party Where My Ex Is?

Go easy, and decide your limit before you arrive.

Alcohol narrows your attention to whatever is right in front of you and pushes out the bigger picture. Researchers call this "alcohol myopia" ([Steele and Josephs, 1990](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.45.8.921)). At a party with your ex, what's right in front of you is them. The bigger picture, the weeks of no contact and the reasons it ended, gets harder to hold onto.

That's how "one drink and a quick catch-up" becomes a 1am heart-to-heart outside, or a text from the ride home.

- Alternate every drink with water or something fizzy.
- Hold a glass of something soft so nobody keeps topping you up.
- Hand your phone to your friend after drink number two, if you trust them.

## When Should I Leave Early?

Leave when your plan says to, or sooner if any of these happen:

- You've checked where they are more than a few times in ten minutes.
- You're rehearsing what you'd say to them.
- You're about to have the drink that goes past your limit.
- You've cried, and you'd rather not do it again in front of everyone.

Leaving early isn't running away. It's the plan working. Skip the round of goodbyes if you need to and text the host a thank-you later.

## What About the Morning After?

You wake up with a dry mouth and a replay of the night. They looked good. They said "Hi, how are you?" in that voice. And now you want to send something small:

> It was nice to see you last night.

Don't. That text isn't about the party. It's a question: did you feel it too? And whatever they reply, or don't, you'll be checking your phone all day. In a study that followed young adults for four weeks after a breakup, people who stayed in contact with their ex saw love and sadness fade more slowly ([Sbarra and Emery, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1350-4126.2005.00112.x)). One friendly text is exactly how that contact starts again.

Instead, call the friend who was with you and talk through it. Write down what you felt. Then let the day be a normal day.

## FAQ: Seeing Your Ex at a Holiday Party

**How should I act when I see my ex at a party?**
Polite, brief and busy. Say hello if they come over, use one short line, and go back to your friends. You don't need to hide from them, and you don't need to prove you're fine.

**Should I go to the work Christmas party if my ex works there?**
Usually yes, at least for the first hour. Treat it like a louder version of a workday: work-only contact, a drink limit and a set time to leave.

**Does saying hi to my ex at a party break no contact?**
No. A short, polite hello in a shared space is fine. Long private talks, swapping numbers or texting afterward are where it breaks.

**Should I text my ex after seeing them at a party?**
No. "It was nice to see you" is usually a way of asking whether they still care. Call a friend or write the message somewhere it won't send instead.
