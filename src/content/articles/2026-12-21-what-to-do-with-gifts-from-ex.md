It's in the closet, still in the bag from the store. A sweater you picked out for them in November, back when December looked different. Gift receipt taped to the tissue paper.

And on your shelf, the candle they gave you last Christmas, the one you never lit because it felt too nice to burn.

December puts gifts everywhere. Here's what to do with the ones from them, and the ones for them.

## Should I Return Gifts From My Ex?

Usually, no. A gift is a gift. Once they gave it to you, it's yours, and you don't owe it back because the relationship ended.

There are a few exceptions where giving it back is the kind or sensible thing:

- **Family heirlooms.** Their grandmother's ring, a watch that's been in the family, anything that belongs to their history more than yours.
- **Things that were clearly shared.** The record player you both used, the dog bed, the "for the apartment" gift.
- **Something very expensive they gave recently,** if keeping it makes you uncomfortable. That's your call, not a rule.

Everything else is up to you. And you don't have to decide this week.

## What Are My Options?

There are five, and you can mix them. Most people keep a few things, box a few and let the rest go.

| Option | Good for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Keep** | Things you'd use anyway: a good coat, a kitchen tool, a book you love | Things you only keep because they came from them |
| **Store** | Anything that hurts to look at but you're not ready to lose | Opening the box every week |
| **Give away** | Things you won't use and can't look at | Giving to mutual friends who'll mention it |
| **Sell** | Valuable things you don't want: jewelry, electronics, designer items | Selling in week one and regretting it later |
| **Return** | Heirlooms, shared things, anything you feel you should | Using the return as a way to see them |

## Why Store First?

Because a breakup makes everything feel urgent, including getting rid of things. Selling the necklace in week one can feel great on the day and terrible a year later.

Seeing their gifts every day also matters more than it seems. When researchers scanned the brains of people who had recently been rejected and were still in love, looking at a photo of the person who left them activated areas linked to reward and craving ([Fisher et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00784.2009)). That study used photos, not objects, but a bracelet you see every morning is a reminder of the same person.

So try this: put every gift that stings into one box, put the box somewhere you don't see every day, and set a reminder for three months from now. Decide then, when you're calmer.

## What Do I Do With the Christmas Gift I Already Bought Them?

This is the December version of the problem, and it's surprisingly common. You shopped early. Then it ended.

Don't give it to them. A gift arriving on their doorstep in December says "I'm still thinking about you," however you wrap it. It reopens the door, and then you spend Christmas waiting to see if they reply.

Instead:

- **Return it to the store** if you can. Check the return window on the receipt now, before it closes.
- **Keep it for yourself** if it's something you'd like. The sweater fits you too? Wear it.
- **Give it to someone else.** A friend, a sibling, a gift swap at work.
- **Donate it** to a holiday gift drive.

And if you bought something for their family (their mom's scarf, their nephew's toy), the same goes. Sending gifts to their family is contact by another route. If you were close, a card can wait until you're sure it's for them and not a message to your ex.

## What About an Engagement Ring?

Engagement rings are the one gift where the usual "it's yours" rule may not apply. In some places, the law treats an engagement ring differently from other gifts, and who keeps it can depend on where you live and how the engagement ended. Rules vary a lot by state and country.

This isn't legal advice. If the ring is valuable and you're not sure, talk to a lawyer where you live before you sell it, return it or argue about it. Whatever you decide, keep the handover as simple as everything else below.

## Why Is "I Have Your Stuff" Such a Classic Reason to Text?

Because it looks like logistics. It's neutral, it's polite, and it gives you a reason to message them that doesn't feel like breaking anything.

But be honest with yourself. If what you're really hoping for is that they'll say "keep it, I want you to have it," or "can we meet to swap?", or anything warmer than a time and an address, then the gift isn't the reason. It's the excuse.

That matters. In a study that followed young adults for four weeks after a breakup, people who stayed in contact with their ex saw love and sadness fade more slowly ([Sbarra and Emery, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1350-4126.2005.00112.x)). A "quick" message about a gift can turn into exactly that kind of contact.

Ask yourself three questions before you send anything:

1. Would I send this if I knew they'd only reply "ok"?
2. Am I returning this because they'd want it, or because I want a reply?
3. Could someone else handle this for me?

## How Do I Return Gifts Without Opening a Conversation?

If you decide to give something back, do it once, and keep it about logistics.

**Batch everything.** One box, one handoff. Several returns mean several conversations.

**Use a go-between if you can.** A friend, a family member, a doorstep drop at an agreed time, or the mail.

**Send one short message with a choice in it.** Not a question about how they are.

> "I have a few of your things, including your grandmother's ring. I can leave them with Maya on Saturday or mail them this week. Which works?"

> "Your watch is in a padded envelope. I'll mail it to your mom's address on Monday unless you'd rather I send it somewhere else."

**Don't put a note in the box.** No card, no "I hope you're well," no letter explaining. If you need to say something, write it where it won't be sent. Our [unsent letter to your ex](/blog/articles/unsent-letter-to-ex/) guide shows how.

**Answer only the logistics.** If they reply with something personal, answer the part about the date and the address, and stop there. Our guide to [what to text your ex](/blog/articles/what-to-text-your-ex/) has more short replies for exactly this.

## What If They Ask for a Gift Back?

That's allowed, and how you reply is up to you. If it's an heirloom or something that was shared, give it back, the same way: one box, one short message, no note.

If it's an ordinary gift and you'd rather keep it, you can say so:

> "I'd like to keep it. I hope that's okay."

Then don't argue. If it turns into a back-and-forth, sending it back is often cheaper than the conversation.

## FAQ: Gifts From Your Ex

**Should I give back gifts after a breakup?**
You don't have to. Gifts are yours. It's kind to return family heirlooms and shared items, and you can return anything else if keeping it makes you uncomfortable.

**What should I do with gifts from my ex?**
Keep what you'd use anyway, box what hurts to see, and give away or sell the rest once you're sure. Waiting a few months before you sell or throw anything out avoids regret.

**What do I do with a Christmas gift I bought for my ex?**
Don't send it. Return it to the store, keep it, give it to someone else or donate it. A gift in December reopens contact, however it's wrapped.

**Do I have to return the engagement ring?**
It depends on where you live. Engagement rings can be treated differently from other gifts, and rules vary by state and country. Talk to a local lawyer if the ring is valuable.

**Is texting my ex to return their stuff breaking no contact?**
Not if it's one short message about logistics. It turns into breaking no contact when the message invites a conversation, like suggesting coffee or asking how they are.
