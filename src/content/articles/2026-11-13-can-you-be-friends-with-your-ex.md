"Let's stay friends." It sounds mature and kind. It can also be the thing that keeps you stuck for months.

So can you be friends with an ex? Sometimes. But it depends on why, and when.

## Why Do People Stay Friends With an Ex?

Researchers at Oakland University asked people why they'd stay friends with a former partner. A first group of 348 people listed reasons, and a second group of people who'd been through a breakup rated how important each one was. The reasons fell into seven groups, including the ex being reliable and trustworthy, practical reasons like money, keeping a shared friend group from getting awkward, still having feelings, children and shared things, and continued sex ([Mogilski and Welling, 2017, via the British Psychological Society](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/reasons-we-stay-friends-ex)).

People rated reliability and sentimental value as the most important reasons, and practical reasons as the least important.

One reason on the list is worth looking at closely: "I still had feelings for them." If that's yours, it's not friendship you're after.

## Friendship or Holding On? A Quick Check

| Sign it could be a real friendship | Sign you're holding on |
| --- | --- |
| Neither of you wants to get back together | You hope friendship leads back to the relationship |
| You've had real time apart first | You went straight from partners to "friends" |
| You'd be fine hearing they're dating | The thought of them dating someone makes you sick |
| You'd be friends even if nothing romantic had happened | The friendship is the only way to stay in their life |
| Talking to them leaves you calm | Every message brings hope, then a crash |

If most of your answers are in the right column, that's normal, and it means you need distance first.

## Why Does My Ex Want to Be Friends?

Sometimes they really value you and want to keep you in their life. Sometimes they feel guilty and want to soften the ending. Sometimes it keeps you available. And sometimes it's just easier than a clean break.

You don't need to work out which. The question is what friendship would do for you right now.

## How Long Should You Wait Before Being Friends?

There's no fixed number, but a few months of no contact is a good start. Our [no contact rule guide](/blog/articles/no-contact-rule-guide/) covers 30, 60 and 90 day timelines.

You'll know it's time when:

- You can see their name without your stomach dropping.
- You're not checking their profile or [missing them every night](/blog/articles/why-breakups-hurt-more-at-night/).
- You'd genuinely be glad, or at least fine, if they were happy with someone else.

If you're not there yet, you can say so kindly: "I'd like to be friends one day, but I need space first. I'll reach out if and when I'm ready."

## When Friendship With an Ex Isn't a Good Idea

- **The relationship was controlling or abusive.** Distance is safer, and "friendship" can be a way back in. See [what is hoovering](/blog/articles/what-is-hoovering/).
- **They cheated or kept lying.** Trust is the main thing friends need.
- **You're the one still in love.** Every coffee becomes a test of whether they feel it too.
- **They keep [breadcrumbing](/blog/articles/what-is-breadcrumbing/) you.** That's not friendship either.
- **A new partner of either of you is uncomfortable** and it keeps causing fights.

## FAQ: Being Friends With an Ex

**Is it possible to be just friends with an ex?**
Yes, for some people, usually after time apart and once neither of you wants more. It's less likely if one of you still has feelings.

**Should I stay friends with my ex right after the breakup?**
Usually not. Going straight from partners to friends keeps the old patterns going. Take time apart first.

**What do I say if my ex asks to be friends?**
"I'd like that one day, but I need space first." You don't have to give a timeline.

**Is wanting to be friends with my ex a sign I'm not over them?**
Not always. But if you want friendship mainly to stay close to them, or hope it will lead back to a relationship, it probably is. Our post on [why you miss your ex](/blog/articles/why-do-i-miss-my-ex/) explains that pull.
