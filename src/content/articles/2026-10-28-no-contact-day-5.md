Five days. You're past the point most people expect to break, and probably still not feeling great about it. That's the strange thing about day 5: the urge to text is often quieter, but your head is louder.

## What Does Day 5 of No Contact Feel Like?

For many people the craving peaks around [day 3](/blog/articles/hardest-day-no-contact/). By day 5 it's usually still there, just less frantic. What tends to take its place:

- **Replaying.** Old conversations, the last fight, the good weekend in the spring. On a loop, often at night.
- **Rewriting.** You argue with them in your head and win this time. Or you script what you'd say if they called.
- **Wondering.** Where they are tonight, who they're talking to, whether they've noticed you've gone quiet.
- **Tiredness.** Five days of running on stress is draining, and a flat, heavy day is common.
- **Small gaps.** A few minutes where you weren't thinking about them, followed by a pang when you notice.

None of this means no contact isn't working. It means the part of you that was busy resisting the urge now has room to think, and it's thinking about them.

## Day 5 at a Glance

| What you might feel | Why it happens | What helps today |
|---|---|---|
| Replaying the same conversations | Your brain is trying to make sense of the ending | Write it out once, then close it |
| A dull, tired sadness | Stress from the first days is catching up | Lower the bar: eat, sleep, get outside once |
| Urges that come and go faster | The day 3 peak is passing | Wait out each one before you decide anything |

| Wondering what they think of your silence | Not knowing is hard to sit with | Turn the question back to what you need |

## Why Is My Brain Replaying Everything?

Because a breakup is a loss your brain is still trying to process. In brain scans of people who had just been rejected by a partner, seeing a photo of the ex lit up reward and craving regions linked to addiction ([Fisher et al., 2010](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/jn.00784.2009)). The wanting doesn't switch off just because the texting has. With no new contact to feed it, your mind replays old material instead.

Replaying isn't a sign you're going backwards. It does need an exit, though, or it can eat whole evenings.

**Give the loop an exit.** Write the conversation you keep replaying, in full, somewhere it can't send. Our free [unsent text tool](/tools/unsent-text/) works for this. Keep it to one go, though. A study of 90 recently separated adults found that people who kept going over and over why it happened did worse months later after emotional writing than after writing about neutral topics ([Sbarra et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/2167702612469801)). Write it to let it out, not to solve it.

**Don't feed it.** Every look at their stories gives the loop new material. In a study of 464 people, those who kept checking an ex's Facebook page reported more distress and more longing for the ex ([Marshall, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1089/cyber.2012.0125)). If that's where you're slipping, read [how to stop checking their Instagram](/blog/articles/stop-checking-ex-instagram/).

## What Is He Thinking on Day 5 of No Contact?

It's one of the most searched questions about day 5, so here's the honest answer: you can't know, and five days is too short to read anything into. Some people feel relief right after a breakup, some feel regret, and plenty feel both. He might not have noticed the silence yet, or he might be checking his phone too.

What you can know is why you're asking. Usually it's hope that he misses you, which turns into "so maybe I should reach out." That's the urge, dressed up as a question. More on this in [what he's thinking during no contact](/blog/articles/what-is-he-thinking-during-no-contact/).

## FAQ: No Contact Day 5

**Is day 5 of no contact hard?**
It can be, in a different way from day 3. The urge to text is often quieter, but replaying and wondering about them get louder. A tired, flat day is common too. All of that is normal.

**Does no contact get easier after 5 days?**
For many people, the urges start to come less often and pass faster somewhere between days 5 and 10. It isn't a straight line, though, and a bad evening on day 6 or 8 doesn't mean you're back at the start.

**What is he thinking after 5 days of no contact?**
Nobody can tell you, and five days is too early to read anything into his silence. He might feel relief, regret or both. No contact is about what you need, not a test of what he feels.

**Should I break no contact on day 5?**
If you're asking because the urge is loud, wait it out first. Urges peak and pass. If you still think there's a real reason to reach out, take the [should I text my ex quiz](/tools/should-i-text-my-ex/) before you send anything.
