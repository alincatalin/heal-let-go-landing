You don't need to be a writer. You don't need a beautiful notebook. You need ten minutes, somewhere private, and a question that gets you started.

This page has 50 of those questions, grouped by where you are in the breakup. And first, a few things research says about how to journal so it actually helps.

## Does Journaling Help After a Breakup?

**Yes, especially when you write about your feelings and what they mean, not just what happened.** In a randomized study, 145 students wrote either about their breakup or about neutral topics. Those who wrote about the breakup avoided the rise in tension, fatigue and physical symptoms that the comparison group had ([Lepore and Greenberg, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1080/08870440290025768)).

Reflecting on a breakup over time also seems to help you rebuild who you are. Young adults who reflected on their breakup repeatedly over nine weeks developed a clearer sense of self, and that was linked to less loneliness and fewer intrusive thoughts about the ex ([Larson and Sbarra, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550614563085)).

There's one caution. In a study of recently separated adults, people who tend to dwell on things did worse when asked to write deeply about their separation ([Sbarra et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/2167702612469801)). Writing helps when it moves you forward. It hurts when it becomes another way to replay the same scene.

| Journaling that helps | Journaling that keeps you stuck |
| --- | --- |
| Naming feelings: "I feel humiliated and lonely" | Re-living the same fight word for word |
| Asking what something means for you | Trying to work out exactly what they're thinking |
| Noticing what you don't miss | Only writing about the best memories |
| Writing a letter you'll never send | Drafting the "perfect" message to actually send |
| 10 to 20 minutes, then close it | Hours a day, every day |
| Looking forward as well as back | Only looking back |

## How to Journal After a Breakup

1. **Set a time limit.** 10 to 20 minutes. Use a timer.
2. **Write by hand or in a private app.** Somewhere nobody else will read it, and nothing can be sent.
3. **Pick one prompt.** Don't try to answer ten at once.
4. **Write without editing.** Spelling, grammar and fairness don't matter here.
5. **End with one line about today.** Something you'll do, notice or be kind to yourself about.
6. **If you're going in circles, stop.** Switch to a prompt from a different section, or close it and go for a walk.

## Prompts for the First Week

When everything is raw, keep it simple.

1. Right now, my body feels...
2. The hardest moment today was... and I got through it by...
3. What I wish someone would say to me is...
4. One thing I need tomorrow is...
5. Who can I lean on this week, and what will I ask them for?
6. What am I afraid of, if I'm completely honest?
7. Something small that helped, even a little, was...
8. If my best friend were going through this, I'd tell them...

## Prompts for Anger and Hurt

Anger is part of it. Give it a page so it doesn't end up in their inbox.

9. What I'm angriest about is...
10. What I never got to say is...
11. The moment I felt most disrespected was...
12. What I'm angry at myself for is... and what I'd say to that version of me is...
13. If my anger could talk, it would want me to protect...
14. What did I put up with that I won't put up with again?
15. Write a letter to them you will never send. Say all of it.

## Prompts for When You Miss Them

16. What exactly am I missing right now: them, the routine, or being with someone?
17. Three things I miss, and three things I definitely don't miss.
18. A moment I'm romanticizing, and what actually happened.
19. What would really change if I texted them tonight?
20. What did I need from the relationship that I wasn't getting?
21. If they came back tomorrow with nothing different, how would that go?
22. What I'd tell my future self who is about to text them...

Thinking about an ex's flaws, honestly and without insults, is one of the few things shown to lower love feelings after a breakup ([Langeslag and Sanchez, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000360)). Prompts 17, 18 and 21 do this. Turn your answers into a lock screen with our free [reasons not to text them](/tools/reasons-not-to-text/) tool.

## Prompts for Understanding What Happened

Use these once the first shock has passed. If they start you replaying, switch sections.

23. In one paragraph: why did it end, really?
24. What patterns did we fall into?
25. What was my part in it? (Fairly, not harshly.)
26. What was theirs?
27. When did I first notice something was wrong, and what did I do?
28. What did I learn about what I need in a relationship?
29. What story am I telling myself about this breakup? Is it the only true one?

## Prompts for Closure

Closure is something you can give yourself. More in [how to get closure after a breakup](/blog/articles/how-to-get-closure/).

30. What question do I keep wishing they would answer? What's the most likely answer?
31. What would I need to believe to let this go?
32. What am I grateful this relationship taught me, even if it hurt?
33. Write a goodbye to the future you imagined together.
34. What can I forgive, for my own sake, even if I never tell them?
35. If this was the last time I wrote about them, what would I want to say?

## Prompts for Rebuilding Yourself

After a breakup, people's sense of who they are often gets blurry ([Slotter et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167209352250)). These help bring it back into focus.

36. Who was I before this relationship?
37. What did I give up for the relationship that I want back?
38. What do I like about myself that has nothing to do with them?
39. What's something I've always wanted to try?
40. Which friendships do I want to invest in again?
41. What does a good day look like for me now?
42. What am I proud of from the last week?

## Prompts for Moving Forward

43. What do I want my life to look like in six months?
44. What do I want in my next relationship? What are my non-negotiables?
45. What red flags will I take seriously next time?
46. What would "being over it" look like, day to day?
47. One small step toward the life I want, this week, is...
48. What would I tell someone who's on day one of this?
49. How have I changed since the breakup?
50. A letter to myself one year from now.

## FAQ: Breakup Journal Prompts

**What should I write in my journal after a breakup?**
Your feelings and what they mean to you, not just what happened. Prompts like "what am I missing right now?" or "what did I lose of myself?" work better than retelling the last argument.

**How often should I journal after a breakup?**
A short session most days works well: 10 to 20 minutes. In the first weeks, daily can help. Later, a few times a week is enough.

**Can journaling make a breakup worse?**
It can, if it turns into replaying the same scenes over and over. If you finish feeling worse every time, switch to the forward-looking prompts or try a different approach for a while.

**Should I write a letter to my ex?**
Yes, if you don't send it. Writing everything you want to say can release the pressure to say it to them. See [unsent letter to your ex](/blog/articles/unsent-letter-to-ex/).

**Is it better to journal by hand or on my phone?**
Either works. Handwriting slows you down, which some people like. A phone is always with you. Just make sure it's somewhere private, and nowhere near your chat with your ex.

**What are good journal prompts for closure?**
"What question do I keep wishing they'd answer, and what's the most likely answer?" and "What would I need to believe to let this go?" are good starts. See the closure section above.
