Maybe it came in a long talk. Maybe it was a text. Maybe you saw it coming, or maybe it hit you out of nowhere. Either way, someone you love just told you they don't want to be with you anymore.

Right now you may feel shocked, humiliated, desperate to fix it, or all three in the same minute. That's normal. Here's what to do next, and what to avoid.

## What Should You Do Right After Getting Dumped?

**In the first hours, protect yourself and say as little as possible.** You can't think clearly in shock, and most of what feels urgent right now isn't.

- **Keep your reply short.** "Okay. I need some time." is enough. You don't have to agree, argue or explain.
- **Don't negotiate.** Promises to change, lists of reasons, "can we try one more time?" rarely work in the moment, and you may regret them later.
- **Get somewhere safe.** Home, a friend's place, anywhere you can fall apart in private.
- **Tell one person.** Text a friend or family member: "I just got dumped. Can you talk?" You shouldn't carry the first night alone.
- **Put the phone down.** Mute their chat for now. The messages you want to send tonight are the ones you're most likely to regret.

If the words won't stop, write them somewhere they can't send. Our free [unsent text tool](/tools/unsent-text/) looks like a chat but never reaches them.

## Why Does Getting Dumped Hurt So Much?

**Because rejection hurts in a way that losing a relationship you chose to end usually doesn't.** You lose the person and you get the message that they didn't want you. Your brain registers both.

In a study of 192 university students, breakup distress was higher in those who hadn't initiated the breakup, who felt rejected, and whose breakup was recent or sudden ([Field et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20432597/)). Brain scans of people after an unwanted breakup show that thinking about the rejection activates some of the same areas involved in physical pain ([Kross et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1102693108)).

| Being the one dumped | Being the one who ended it |
| --- | --- |
| Shock, often no time to prepare | Had time to think and decide |
| Feels like rejection of you | Feels more like a loss and guilt |
| Hard to make sense of their reasons | Knows their own reasons |
| Strong urge to win them back | Urge to check they're okay, or doubt |
| Hope that they'll change their mind | Relief mixed with sadness |

For more on why it hurts this much, see [why breakups hurt so much](/blog/articles/why-do-breakups-hurt-so-much/).

## What Not to Do After Getting Dumped

These all feel tempting. They almost always make things worse:

- **Begging or pleading.** It rarely changes their mind, and it can leave you feeling worse about yourself.
- **Sending a long message.** The essay explaining everything is usually better as an [unsent letter](/blog/articles/unsent-letter-to-ex/).
- **Showing up unannounced.** It puts them on the defensive and can turn a sad ending into a painful scene.
- **Drunk texting.** Archive their chat before you go out, or give your phone to a friend.
- **Posting about it.** Cryptic quotes, revenge selfies and sad songs are messages to them in disguise.
- **Checking their profile all day.** Every check is a fresh hit of hurt. Mute them now. Our guide on [how to stop checking their Instagram](/blog/articles/stop-checking-ex-instagram/) helps.
- **Jumping into a rebound to prove a point.** If you're dating to get a reaction, it's not about the new person. See [rebound relationships](/blog/articles/rebound-relationship/).

If you've already done some of these, that's okay. Stop where you are and start again from today. Our post on [what to do after you break no contact](/blog/articles/i-broke-no-contact/) explains how.

## How Do You Get Over Being Dumped?

### The first week

- **Go into no contact.** Mute, archive, and if you need to, [block them](/blog/articles/should-i-block-my-ex/) for now. You can unblock later. Our [no contact rule guide](/blog/articles/no-contact-rule-guide/) explains why the space helps.
- **Handle the practical stuff once.** If you live together or share things, sort it in one short, calm exchange, ideally in writing.
- **Look after the basics.** Eat something, drink water, get daylight, try to keep a regular wake-up time. If nights are bad, read [can't sleep after a breakup](/blog/articles/cant-sleep-after-a-breakup/).
- **Let people in.** Say yes when friends offer. Tell them what helps: company, distraction, or just someone to listen.

### The first month

- **Stop asking why on a loop.** Write your questions down once, along with your best honest answers. Then close the notebook. Our guide on [how to get closure](/blog/articles/how-to-get-closure/) walks through it.
- **Separate their choice from your worth.** Their decision says what they wanted and could give. It doesn't decide whether you're lovable.
- **Notice what you don't miss.** The things you put up with, the parts of you that got smaller. Our [reasons not to text your ex tool](/tools/reasons-not-to-text/) helps you keep the list.
- **Do one thing a day that's only yours.** A walk, a class, a meal with someone. You're rebuilding a life that doesn't revolve around them.

### After that

Most people notice the sharpest pain easing over weeks to a few months. It comes in waves, not a straight line. Our post on [how long it takes to get over a breakup](/blog/articles/how-long-does-it-take-to-get-over-a-breakup/) covers what to expect, and [how to accept a breakup you didn't want](/blog/articles/how-to-accept-a-breakup/) helps with the hope that keeps coming back.

## Should You Try to Get Them Back?

**Not right now.** In the first weeks you're in shock, and almost any move you make will come from panic, not a clear choice. Give it at least 30 days of real space first.

After that, many people find they want something different. ## FAQ: Getting Dumped

**What should I say when I get dumped?**
Very little. Something like "Okay. I'm hurt, and I need some time. Please don't contact me for a while." You don't owe them a debate, and you don't need to agree with their reasons.

**How long does it take to get over being dumped?**
There's no fixed time. Many people feel the worst of it ease within a few weeks to a few months, especially with no contact. It often takes longer when the breakup was sudden or you keep hoping they'll change their mind.

**Is it normal to want them back right after getting dumped?**
Yes. Wanting them back is one of the most common reactions, partly because your brain craves the person like a habit. It doesn't mean getting back together is the right choice. Give it time before you decide anything.

**Why do I feel so humiliated after getting dumped?**
Because rejection stings your sense of worth, not just your heart. It helps to remember that their choice reflects what they wanted, not a verdict on you. Talking to friends who know you well helps too.

**Should I stay friends after getting dumped?**
Not straight away. Staying friends right after being dumped usually keeps hope alive. If friendship is possible, it tends to work only much later, once you truly don't want more. More in [can you be friends with your ex](/blog/articles/can-you-be-friends-with-your-ex/).

**What if I got dumped over text?**
It's okay to feel extra hurt by that. You don't have to reply right away. When you do, keep it short. You don't need a call or a meeting to start healing.
