Everyone has breakup advice. Your mom, your best friend, the internet. Some of it helps. Some of it ("just get under someone new") really doesn't.

Here's the advice that holds up, based on what research says about how people actually recover, grouped by when you'll need it. Each tip links to a deeper guide if you want more.

## What Is the Best Breakup Advice?

**Create distance from your ex, let yourself grieve, and look after the basics while time does its work.** Almost everything that helps is a version of one of those three. Almost everything that hurts (checking their profile, "one last talk," numbing it) undoes one of them.

## Breakup Advice for the First Few Days

**1. Don't try to fix it right now.** Begging, bargaining and long messages in the first days almost never change the outcome, and you may regret them. If you were the one left, read [getting dumped](/blog/articles/getting-dumped/).

**2. Tell one person.** Don't carry the first night alone. People who leaned on friends and family coped better in a survey of over 5,000 people about their breakups ([Davis, Shaver and Vernon, 2003](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167203252884)).

**3. Mute them before you're tempted.** Archive the chat, mute their stories, move the photos. Do it now, while you're thinking clearly. Unsure whether to block? See [should I block my ex](/blog/articles/should-i-block-my-ex/).

**4. Eat something and sleep when you can.** It sounds too basic to matter. It matters. Our [what to do after a breakup](/blog/articles/what-to-do-after-a-breakup/) guide has a first-week plan.

## Breakup Advice for When You Want to Text Them

**5. Wait an hour, then a day.** Most urges peak and pass. Waiting is the simplest thing that works.

**6. Write it, don't send it.** The urge is usually about being heard, not about their reply. Type it in our [unsent text tool](/tools/unsent-text/), or write an [unsent letter](/blog/articles/unsent-letter-to-ex/).

**7. Take space for real.** In a study that tracked young adults after a breakup, sadness and love for the ex faded more slowly in those who stayed in contact ([Sbarra and Emery, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1350-4126.2005.00112.x)). That's the whole idea behind [no contact](/blog/articles/no-contact-rule-guide/).

**8. Ask what you're hoping for.** Before sending anything, take our [should I text my ex quiz](/tools/should-i-text-my-ex/). If they've already texted you, read [what to do when your ex texts you](/blog/articles/my-ex-texted-me/).

## Breakup Advice for the Bad Days and Nights

**9. Let the sadness in, in doses.** Trying not to think about something tends to make it return more ([Wegner et al., 1987](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.53.1.5)). Give it ten or twenty minutes, then do something that needs your attention.

**10. Remember the whole relationship.** Write down what didn't work. Focusing on an ex's downsides lowered love feelings in one study, even if mood dipped for a while ([Langeslag and Sanchez, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000360)). Our [reasons not to text your ex tool](/tools/reasons-not-to-text/) helps you keep the list.

**11. Have a night plan.** Nights are often the hardest part. Phone out of the bedroom, one calming routine, one person you can text. See [why breakups hurt more at night](/blog/articles/why-breakups-hurt-more-at-night/) and [can't sleep after a breakup](/blog/articles/cant-sleep-after-a-breakup/).

**12. Know when it's more than heartbreak.** If low mood lasts most of the day for two weeks or more, or you can't function, talk to a doctor. Read [depression after a breakup](/blog/articles/depression-after-a-breakup/). If you have thoughts of hurting yourself, reach out now: in the US, call or text 988, or find a free helpline at [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com).

## Breakup Advice for Moving On

**13. Rebuild who you are.** After a breakup, your sense of self often gets blurry, and that's linked to more distress ([Slotter, Gardner and Finkel, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167209352250)). Do things that are only yours: a class, a trip, old friends. Our guide on [how to get over a breakup](/blog/articles/how-to-get-over-a-breakup/) walks through it.

**14. Don't rush into a rebound to prove something.** Dating can be fine when you're ready. Dating to forget, or to make them jealous, rarely helps. See [rebound relationships](/blog/articles/rebound-relationship/) and [dating after a breakup](/blog/articles/dating-after-a-breakup/).

**15. Give it time, and count it.** Most people feel the worst pain ease over weeks to a few months, with waves after. Counting the days you didn't reach out is proof you're doing it. Use our free [no contact counter](/tools/no-contact-counter/).

## Breakup Advice to Ignore

Some popular advice sounds good and backfires:

- **"Just get under someone new."** Sometimes it boosts confidence. Often it just delays the grief.
- **"Stay friends right away."** Friendship can work later, rarely straight after. See [can you be friends with your ex](/blog/articles/can-you-be-friends-with-your-ex/).
- **"Do no contact to make them come back."** No contact is for you. Treating it as a strategy keeps you waiting. Read [will my ex come back](/blog/articles/will-my-ex-come-back/) for an honest look.
- **"Don't cry, they're not worth it."** Crying isn't weakness. It's part of letting go.
- **"Just stay busy."** Busy helps. Never stopping to feel it means it waits for you.
- **"Get closure from them."** It rarely comes from them. Here's [how to get closure on your own](/blog/articles/how-to-get-closure/).

## Should You Get Back Together?

Not in the first weeks. Give it at least 30 days of real space, then decide when you're calm. Our guide on [should I get back with my ex](/blog/articles/should-i-get-back-with-my-ex/) helps you tell the difference between missing someone and the relationship being right.

## FAQ: Breakup Advice

**What is the first thing to do after a breakup?**
Get somewhere safe, tell one person you trust, and mute your ex's chat and profiles. Don't try to fix it or decide anything big in the first days.

**What's the best advice for getting over someone?**
Take real space, let yourself grieve, and keep your days full of people and small routines. Feelings fade with time and distance, especially without contact. See [how to stop missing someone](/blog/articles/how-to-stop-missing-someone/).

**Is breakup advice different for men and women?**
The core advice is the same. Research suggests women report slightly more pain at first and recover more fully, while men may process it later. Read [how to get over him](/blog/articles/how-to-get-over-him/) or [how to get over her](/blog/articles/how-to-get-over-her/).

**What should you not do after a breakup?**
Don't beg, drunk text, check their profile all day, post at them, or jump into a rebound to prove a point. If you slip, start again from today. See [what to do after you break no contact](/blog/articles/i-broke-no-contact/).

**How do I help a friend with a breakup?**
Listen more than you advise, offer specific help, and keep checking in for weeks. Our guide on [how to help a friend through a breakup](/blog/articles/how-to-help-a-friend-through-a-breakup/) has what to say and what to avoid.
