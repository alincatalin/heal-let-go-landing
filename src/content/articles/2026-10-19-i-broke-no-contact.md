You sent it. Maybe it was "hey," maybe it was three paragraphs at 1am. Now your stomach has dropped and you're staring at the screen.

First, breathe. This happens to almost everyone who tries no contact. It doesn't mean you're weak, and it doesn't mean you have to start your healing from nothing.

## Did I Ruin Everything?

No. The days you went without contact still happened. You still got through them, and you still learned you can. (Not sure a like or a story view even counts? See [what counts as breaking no contact](/blog/articles/what-counts-as-breaking-no-contact/).)

What a slip does is reopen the loop for a bit. In a study that followed young adults through the month after a breakup, people who stayed in contact with their ex saw their sadness and love for the ex fade more slowly ([Sbarra and Emery, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1350-4126.2005.00112.x)). One message isn't the same as staying in touch. But it's why the next step matters more than the slip itself.

## What Should I Do in the Next Hour?

Keep it simple. You're trying not to turn one message into a conversation.

1. **Don't send a second message.** No "sorry, ignore that," no follow-up explaining the first one.
2. **Put the phone in another room.** Waiting for the typing dots is its own kind of pain.
3. **Tell one person.** A friend who knows, so you're not alone with it.
4. **Write the rest down somewhere it can't send.** Whatever else you wanted to say still needs somewhere to go.
5. **Go to sleep, or do one small physical thing.** A shower, a walk, a glass of water.

## Should I Reply If They Answer?

Only if there's something practical to answer. If they reply with logistics, answer the logistics in one line and stop.

If they reply warmly, it will feel like relief. If they reply coldly, or not at all, it will sting. Either way, you don't have to keep it going. "I shouldn't have texted. I'm going to take some space" is a complete reply.

## Why Did I Break It?

Most slips have a pattern, and it's usually not random. Look back at the moment:

| What happened just before | What it usually means | What to plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| It was late at night | You were tired and alone with it | Phone out of the bedroom after 11pm |
| You'd been drinking | Your guard was down | Archive the chat before you go out |
| You saw a photo or post | Something reopened the wound | Mute them, and anyone who posts them |
| A date or anniversary | The day carried the memory | Plan that day in advance, with people |
| You felt really good | You told yourself you were "fine now" | Wait a week before testing it |

There's no shame in any of these. They're just the moments to plan for.

## How Do I Stop It Happening Again?

Pick your one riskiest moment from the table above and plan just for that one. Not every scenario, just the most likely.

Then make the next slip harder: archive the chat, mute their profiles, and keep something to write in for when the urge hits. ## FAQ: Breaking No Contact

**Does breaking no contact reset everything?**
You start counting from today, but you don't lose what the earlier days gave you. One message doesn't undo weeks of distance. What matters more is not following it with a second one.

**I texted my ex during no contact. Should I apologize?**
Usually not. An apology opens another round of conversation. If you must say something, keep it to one line: "I shouldn't have texted. I'm taking some space."

**Does it count as breaking no contact if they texted first?**
Replying counts as contact. Their message isn't your slip. If you do answer, keep it to anything practical and stop there.

**What if I keep breaking no contact?**
Look for the pattern: the time of day, drinking, a trigger on social media. Plan for that one moment and make contact harder, for example by archiving the chat. If you can't stop at all, it may help to talk it through with a therapist.
