Short answer: **Probably not right now.**

Longer answer: It depends on why you want to text them, what you hope to get back, and whether you're ready for any response (or no response at all).

If you're reading this at 2am with your phone in your hand and your ex's name pulled up, take a breath. You're not weak. You're not crazy. And you're definitely not alone.

## The 2am Panic

We’ve all been there. The silence of the night makes the noise in your head louder. You pull up their name. You look at old photos. You think about that one thing you should have said.

But before you hit send, ask yourself: **What is the goal of this message?**

If the goal is to stop the pain, a text is only a temporary band-aid. And often, it’s a band-aid that rips off even more skin.

## When It's Okay to Reach Out

There are very few times when reaching out is a good idea in the early stages of a breakup. Usually, it's only for:

1. Logistics (getting your stuff back)
2. Legal matters
3. Shared responsibilities (kids, pets, business)

If it’s anything else, it’s probably a "breadcrumb" or a "withdrawal" message.

## The "Withdrawal" Text

Your brain is literally going through withdrawal. Romantic rejection activates the same pathways as drug withdrawal. You are looking for a "hit" of dopamine.

The problem is that once the hit wears off, you feel worse than before.

## How to Test Your Message

Before you send *anything*, try this:

1. Write the message in a notes app.
2. Wait 24 hours.
3. If you still want to send it, ask a friend to read it.
4. Better yet, use the **Heal** app's "Fake Text" feature. Send it there. See how it feels. Let the AI respond to you and give you the perspective you need without the risk of breaking no contact.

Remember: **Silence is also a message.** And right now, it’s the most powerful one you have.
